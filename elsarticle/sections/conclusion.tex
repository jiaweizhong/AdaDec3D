\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We systematically characterized decoder computation in 3D medical image segmentation. By evaluating matched full and efficient decoder pairs across multiple architectures and datasets, we found that additional decoder computation does not uniformly or monotonically improve accuracy. Instead, it generates substantial, bidirectional correctness changes that are strongly localized near anatomical boundaries. Separately, at least 80\% of positive gain is concentrated within the top 5\% of spatial blocks ($K_{80} \le 5\%$). While inference-time uncertainty reliably locates where these changes occur, it is fundamentally insufficient for determining their directional net benefit. Consequently, these findings establish that future utility-aware decoders must separate candidate-region proposal from explicit expected-gain prediction, and treat high-resolution local refinement as an optional, task-dependent intervention rather than a universally required execution stage.
