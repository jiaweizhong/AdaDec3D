\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Objectives.}
Using the matched \fullmodel{}/\effimodel{} comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:study}, our analysis establishes where the full decoder's benefit
is concentrated, how large and how bidirectional it is, and whether the beneficial
regions can be identified at test time from the efficient model's uncertainty ---
the points left unresolved by aggregate efficient-decoder evaluation.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports these in turn, preceded by the
controlled-reimplementation baselines.

\paragraph{Dataset and split.}
We use the 13-organ BTCV/Synapse abdominal CT task with 18 training and 12
validation volumes.  The evaluated organs are spleen, kidneys, gallbladder,
esophagus, liver, stomach, aorta, inferior vena cava, portal/splenic veins,
pancreas, and adrenal glands.  Images are intensity-windowed and resampled
according to the public EffiDec3D training pipeline; training uses $96^3$
patches.  The same validation subjects are paired across all model comparisons.

\paragraph{Models.}
\fullmodel{} is the public full 3D UX-Net configuration with feature widths
$[48,96,192,384]$.  \effimodel{} uses the corresponding EffiDec3D decoder with
48 channels, additive skip aggregation, and resolution factor two.  Both are
trained for 45k iterations with Dice--cross-entropy loss and AdamW.  We evaluate
one \fullmodel{} run and two independently initialized \effimodel{} runs.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Segmentation quality is reported using mean and per-organ Dice and 95th
percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95).  Efficiency measurements include
parameters, MACs, latency, peak inference memory, and training time.
Sliding-window inference uses a $96^3$ region, batch size four, overlap 0.7,
and constant blending.  Observation confidence intervals resample subjects.

\paragraph{Reimplementation status.}
Our data were reconstructed from the public TransUNet/Synapse release, whose
NIfTI files have identity affine metadata, whereas the EffiDec3D paper refers
to a processed \texttt{btcv\_trns} dataset.  Consequently, we present the
experiment as a \emph{controlled reimplementation}, not an exact reproduction.
All comparisons within our study use the same local data and evaluation code.
We report the discrepancy from published numbers in full.

\paragraph{Architecture generality.}
We organize architecture coverage as a predeclared ladder rather than treating
one additional model as evidence of universality.  The primary causal comparison
is the matched full/efficient 3D UX-Net pair (large-kernel CNN).  A matched
Swin UNETR/EffiDec3D pair tests whether the transition pattern transfers to a
hierarchical Transformer.  UNETR provides a ViT architecture control, and
nnU-Net provides a strong self-configuring CNN baseline.  Unless a matched
EffiDec3D counterpart is implemented under the same protocol, UNETR and nnU-Net
are used only to compare error and spatial-concentration patterns; differences
between independently trained models are not attributed specifically to the
decoder.

\paragraph{Dataset generality.}
The original EffiDec3D evaluation covers FeTA, BTCV, and all ten Medical
Segmentation Decathlon (MSD) tasks.  We preselect three to four representative
tasks from that scope: BTCV abdominal CT as the primary multi-organ task; FeTA
fetal-brain MRI for modality and anatomy shift; MSD Task01 BrainTumour for
multimodal lesion segmentation; and either MSD Task06 Lung or Task08
HepaticVessel as a sparse-lesion or thin-structure stress test.  Every dataset
uses the same positive, negative, and net transition definitions, spatial
concentration analysis, opportunity curve, and subject-level confidence
intervals.  The final set is frozen before inspecting results.
