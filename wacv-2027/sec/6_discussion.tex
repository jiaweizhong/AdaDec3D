\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
Across two efficient-decoder seeds, the direction of the central observation is
stable: the full decoder's aggregate benefit is small and bidirectional, and its
net benefit rises with the efficient model's uncertainty.  Boundary and organ
analyses show that difficulty is spatially heterogeneous, and the region-level
opportunity curve shows that entropy-selected contiguous regions carry
disproportionate net benefit.  Together these justify testing selective regional
decoding rather than a uniformly expensive decoder.

\paragraph{What it does not yet support.}
The study does not demonstrate lower \emph{executed} compute for an adaptive
model.  The opportunity curve uses full-model predictions to define benefit
retrospectively and does not account for errors introduced by a learned
refinement module; the voxel-level $86.4\%$ recovery figure is an oracle upper
bound, not a realizable router.  Dynamic FLOPs, latency, memory, and final Dice
must be evaluated in a separate method study.  The analysis is also
\emph{decoder-only} by construction: because \method{} alters the decoder while
holding the encoder identical, every transition isolates decoder capacity, but
the study says nothing about the encoder, which accounts for the larger share of
compute.  Whether encoder computation is similarly over-provisioned is a distinct
question requiring representational probes rather than a transition pair, and is
left to future work.

\paragraph{Reproduction gap.}
Our efficient baseline remains $1.70$--$2.25$ Dice points below the published
result, while the full baseline is much closer.  The most plausible unresolved
difference is preprocessing: our reconstructed files have identity affine
metadata, whereas the original work used a processed dataset not distributed with
the code.  We tested the obvious remedy---disabling spatial resampling---and it
was decisively worse ($\approx\!10$ Dice points), so bypassing resampling is not
the fix.  The enlarged local full--efficient gap could inflate the apparent
amount of decoder opportunity; we mitigate this by disclosing all values,
analyzing two seeds, and limiting claims to controlled local comparisons and net
(not positive-only) transitions.

\paragraph{Statistical limitations.}
The validation set contains only twelve subjects, so confidence intervals are
wide and millions of voxels must not be treated as independent.  The
entropy--error coefficient we report ($r=0.97$) pools bins across subjects and is
retained only as a descriptive diagnostic; final error predictability should use
subject-level AUROC/AUPRC and calibration.

\paragraph{Generalization.}
All completed results use one CT dataset and one backbone.  Without
cross-backbone and cross-modality replication, the finding may be specific to 3D
UX-Net, the Synapse split, or \method{}'s resolution restriction.  The planned
ladder uses matched pairs across the \method{} backbone family (3D UX-Net and
Swin UNETR, optionally SwinUNETR-V2 and MedNeXt) and representative tasks from
the original \method{} scope; we restrict it to backbones \method{} actually
equips with an efficient decoder, since a matched transition comparison is
undefined otherwise.  The final claim will be narrowed if these experiments do
not reproduce the direction.

\paragraph{Clinical interpretation.}
High uncertainty and small-organ difficulty do not imply clinical safety.
Entropy can miss confidently wrong predictions, and improving average Dice does
not guarantee preservation of clinically important boundaries.  Any future
adaptive decoder should retain a conservative fallback and be evaluated per organ
and per subject, not only by average compute.
