\section{Where the Full Decoder Helps}
\label{sec:study}

\subsection{Matched Decoder Comparison}

Let $f_{\mathrm{E}}$ denote the efficient decoder and $f_{\mathrm{F}}$ the full
decoder.  For input volume $x$, their hard predictions at voxel $v$ are
$\hat y_{\mathrm{E}}(v)$ and $\hat y_{\mathrm{F}}(v)$, with reference label
$y(v)$.  The positive and negative transitions are
\begin{align}
P(v) &= \mathbb{1}[\hat y_{\mathrm{E}}(v)\ne y(v)]
        \mathbb{1}[\hat y_{\mathrm{F}}(v)=y(v)],\\
N(v) &= \mathbb{1}[\hat y_{\mathrm{E}}(v)=y(v)]
        \mathbb{1}[\hat y_{\mathrm{F}}(v)\ne y(v)].
\end{align}
A positive transition is a voxel the full decoder improves; a negative
transition is one it degrades.  We define the discrete net transition
as
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{net}}(v)=P(v)-N(v).
\label{eq:net_transition}
\end{equation}
This prevents every model disagreement from being counted as an improvement.
For a selected region $S$, normalized net recovery is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{net}}(S)=
\frac{\sum_{v\in S} T_{\mathrm{net}}(v)}
     {\sum_v P(v)}.
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
The denominator makes positive recovery interpretable while allowing negative
values when selection degrades more predictions than it improves.  We report
positive and negative components alongside their difference.

\subsection{Difficulty, Transitions, and Predictability}

We separate three maps that are often conflated:
\emph{error} indicates where \effimodel{} is wrong;
\emph{uncertainty} is computed from its predictive distribution; and
\emph{transitions} indicate where replacing its output with \fullmodel{} changes
correctness.  Error concentration alone does not establish an opportunity for
additional decoding.

For \effimodel{} class probabilities $p_c(v)$, predictive entropy is
\begin{equation}
H(v)=-\sum_c p_c(v)\log(p_c(v)+\epsilon).
\end{equation}
We measure the association between entropy and net transitions within each
subject and aggregate subject-level coefficients with a nonparametric bootstrap.
The final analysis will additionally report subject-level AUROC/AUPRC and
calibration for predicting positive and net-beneficial regions.

\subsection{Selective-Allocation Opportunity}

We evaluate budgets $\mathcal{B}=\{5,10,20,30,50\}\%$ in two settings.
First, entropy ranks individual foreground-union voxels, yielding a
non-deployable oracle upper bound.  Second, the volume is partitioned into
$16^3$ blocks, each scored by mean entropy.  Selected blocks receive a
four-voxel context halo; the halo-expanded volume is recorded as an executed
volume proxy.  Net transitions are evaluated on each selected core, reflecting a
patch-based refiner that consumes context but writes only its core prediction.

At each budget, entropy selection is compared with 100 matched random
selections.  Confidence is a second zero-forward-pass baseline.  The primary
budget is declared as 20\%; 10\% and 30\% are sensitivity analyses.  A paired
bootstrap resamples the same subject indices for entropy and random policies.
The opportunity criterion is met only if mean region-level net transition rate is
positive and the lower 95\% confidence bound for entropy minus random exceeds
zero at the primary budget.

\subsection{Anatomical and Training Analyses}

We compare errors at a three-voxel foreground boundary band and eroded
interior.  Per-organ Dice, entropy, volume, and error characterize anatomical
heterogeneity.  Spearman correlation measures the relation between organ size
and difficulty; a partial correlation tests whether entropy explains
difficulty beyond log organ volume.  Finally, checkpoints at 5k, 10k, 20k,
30k, and 45k iterations measure how uncertainty changes during training.
