\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a systematic voxel-level characterization of 3D decoder computation in medical image segmentation, evaluating matched full and efficient decoder pairs across diverse backbones, clinical datasets, controlled factor attributions, and two distinct decoder-efficiency mechanisms (capacity removal and a capacity-preserving factorization). Our findings establish a fundamental distinction between two quantities that aggregate metrics conflate: \textit{correctness activity} (where decoder capacity alters predictions) and \textit{directional net gain} (the signed balance of corrections and degradations). Across architectural families and modalities, high-capacity decoders exhibit extensive gross activity that is strongly counterbalanced by bidirectional cancellation, rendering aggregate volume-wide net gain statistically near-zero. Useful decoder computation is highly concentrated ($K_{80} \le 5\%$) and localized almost exclusively within a narrow $0\text{--}2\text{ mm}$ anatomical boundary zone. Crucially, while predictive uncertainty reliably locates \textit{where} computation alters predictions ($\text{Location AUROC} \ge 0.865$), it fails to discriminate \textit{whether} those alterations improve accuracy ($\text{Direction AUROC} \approx 0.520\text{--}0.591$), uncovering a fundamental Activity--Direction Predictability Gap.

These empirical insights motivate a paradigm shift in 3D network design. Traditional dense, symmetric U-Net decoders allocate high-resolution computation uniformly across the volumetric grid, spending substantial compute in organ interiors where its net utility is indistinguishable from zero. Our results argue instead for decoupled, two-stage architectures: a globally lightweight base decoder that generates coarse volume segmentation, coupled to a sparse, conditionally executed high-resolution refinement pathway invoked selectively at ambiguous boundary patches. Realizing this vision requires moving beyond uncertainty-guided spatial proposal to develop true \textit{directional utility estimators} that anticipate expected net accuracy gain.

Clinically, our characterization provides a principled stratification for model deployment rather than a uniform verdict. Global decoder simplification can be safely adopted for high-throughput screening, intraoperative guidance, and bulk organ volumetry, maximizing inference speed without compromising clinical accuracy. Conversely, heavy decoding capacity should be sparsely targeted to critical anatomical margins---such as tumor margins, vascular walls, and small glandular structures---where small boundary shifts govern high-stakes surgical and radiotherapeutic decisions. By aligning computation with anatomical utility rather than uniform spatial grids, this work provides a foundation for 3D segmentation architectures that are both computationally efficient and clinically dependable.
