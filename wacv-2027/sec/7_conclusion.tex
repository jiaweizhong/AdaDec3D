\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a transition-count perspective on efficient 3D segmentation
decoders.  A controlled full-versus-efficient comparison, holding the encoder
fixed, shows that additional decoder capacity has a small and bidirectional
aggregate effect, that its remaining benefit is concentrated at boundaries and
small structures, and that this benefit is predictable at test time: entropy-
ranked contiguous regions recover the net benefit far above matched-random
selection at a predeclared budget, with a positive paired lower bound.  These
trends are stable across two efficient-decoder seeds despite an unresolved
absolute reproduction gap.

More broadly, the results show why aggregate Dice and FLOPs are insufficient for
understanding efficient decoder design: error, uncertainty, and decoder-specific
benefit are distinct quantities, and only their separation reveals that efficient
decoders are over-provisioned \emph{non-uniformly}.  The net-transition,
paired-bootstrap, and contiguous-region protocol provides a stricter test of
whether selective allocation is empirically justified, and an evaluation template
that other efficient-decoder studies can reuse.

We deliberately stop short of proposing an adaptive method: whether the observed
opportunity can be realized as measured compute savings, and whether the untouched
encoder is similarly over-provisioned, are left to future work.
