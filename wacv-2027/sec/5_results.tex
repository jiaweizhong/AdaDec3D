\subsection{Heterogeneity Metrics}
\label{sec:heterogeneity}

With the parameter-matched \effimodel{}, the full decoder's benefit is small and
\emph{bidirectional to the point of cancellation}.  On 3D UX-Net, \fullmodel{} corrects
$0.278\%$ of \effimodel{}'s voxels ($R_{\mathrm{pos}}$) while degrading $0.232\%$
($R_{\mathrm{neg}}$), so the net flip rate $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is only $0.047\%$ and its
subject-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.020\%, +0.109\%]$ \emph{includes zero}
(Table~\ref{tab:flips}); on Swin UNETR the two rates are equal ($0.201\%$/$0.201\%$) and
$R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is $-0.001\%$ (CI $[-0.045\%, +0.040\%]$).  Across both matched backbones the full decoder's net
benefit is thus \emph{statistically indistinguishable from zero at the voxel level}.
Crucially, this net neutrality coexists with the $1$--$2$ point Dice gap of
Table~\ref{tab:arch}: the flips, though nearly balanced in count, concentrate at organ
boundaries that Dice weights heavily, so the full decoder still moves macro metrics
through a thin boundary band even as its net contribution to voxel correctness vanishes.
Reporting positive flips alone would overstate a voxel-level benefit that, netted against
its own regressions, is essentially none.

This near-zero global net is \emph{not} a uniform absence of effect but a spatially
structured cancellation (H2).  Resolving $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ by distance to the GT
boundary shows the benefit is sharply boundary-localized: on 3D UX-Net it peaks at the
surface ($+0.085$, CI $[+0.070,+0.101]$, at $0$--$1$ vox) and is zero or slightly
\emph{negative} beyond one voxel; on Swin UNETR it is positive across the first two
voxels ($+0.015$/$+0.021$) and negative in the interior ($-0.015$ at ${>}4$ vox).  Either
way the balanced flips of Table~\ref{tab:flips} live in a one-to-two-voxel shell---the
same band Dice weights heavily.  Per organ, the effect is genuinely
\emph{heterogeneous}---some structures gain and others lose (e.g.\ on 3D UX-Net the left
kidney is $+0.061$ but the left adrenal is $-0.071$)---but there is \emph{no robust size
law}: the organ-size/net-flip Spearman flips sign across backbones ($+0.02$ on 3D UX-Net,
$-0.58$ on Swin), so we report the heterogeneity without claiming smaller structures
systematically gain (Fig.~\ref{fig:organ}); the per-organ Dice change
$\Delta\mathrm{Dice}_c$ follows the same per-organ pattern, and surface Dice (NSD) is
$0.741$/$0.845$ at $\tau{=}1$/$2$~mm.  This boundary concentration should exceed a
same-architecture null pair (Full seed~0 vs.\ seed~1) \todo{null-pair floor}, ruling out
seed noise.  (The error-based boundary ratio---\effimodel{} error $4.39\times$ the
interior rate---is a consistent but weaker error-side view, reported in the appendix.)

\subsection{Concentration Metrics}
\label{sec:concentration}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{figures/O_pareto.png}
\caption{C1/P2: net benefit recovered vs.\ the fraction of $16^3$ blocks selected. The
true-net \emph{oracle} (black; C1) upper-bounds concentration, while deployable signals
(entropy, confidence, boundary) and a matched-random selector (P2) show whether that
opportunity is recoverable at test time. Shaded bands are subject $95\%$ CIs.}
\label{fig:concentration}
\end{figure}

We next ask whether that small net benefit is at least spatially concentrated (C1).
Ranking contiguous $16^3$ blocks by their true net benefit $B(r)=\sum_{v\in r}U(v)$ and
normalizing by the positive net mass $\sum_r\max(B(r),0)$, the top $10\%$ of blocks
recover ${>}99\%$ and the top $20\%$ recover $100\%$ of it, with only $K_{80}=5\%$ of
blocks needed to reach $80\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}, oracle curve).  The coverage
curve is thus extremely steep---the residual net benefit \emph{is} concentrated---but it
is concentrated over an \emph{absolute} total that is itself near zero: the signed net
mass is only ${\sim}6.1$k voxels on 3D UX-Net and $-1.3$k (i.e.\ \emph{negative}) on Swin
UNETR.  Reporting coverage and absolute net together is precisely what prevents a steep
curve over a ${\approx}0$ (or negative) total from being read as a large payoff.  For
reference, a voxel-level oracle over entropy captures $83.1\%$ of the \emph{positive}
flips in its top $20\%$; but positive-flip coverage ignores the near-equal negative flips
that cancel them, which is why the signed net-benefit oracle above is the honest
concentration measure.

The scarce flips, though net-neutral, are not random---but their \emph{location} is far
more predictable than their \emph{direction} from the efficient model's own uncertainty
(P1, Table~\ref{tab:pred}).  Entropy separates any correctness-changing flip from stable
voxels (AUROC $0.901$, CI $[0.878,0.920]$) and predicts the \emph{positive}-flip voxel
with per-subject AUROC $0.903$ ($[0.876,0.926]$)~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}.  Restricted
to the flip voxels themselves, however, entropy barely separates an improvement from a
regression: the positive-vs-negative (direction) AUROC is only $0.593$ ($[0.560,0.627]$)
on 3D UX-Net and $0.561$ ($[0.512,0.609]$) on Swin UNETR---weakly above chance and far
below the $0.90$ location AUROC.  Predicting \emph{instability} is easy; predicting
\emph{improvement} is close to uninformative---uncertainty flags where the decoder acts,
not where it helps.  (Entropy is moderately calibrated,
ECE $0.088$, $[0.065,0.115]$~\cite{guo2017calibration}; the pooled-bin entropy--error
correlation $r=0.97$ is a descriptive diagnostic only.)

This weakly predictable direction directly bounds deployable region selection (P2).
Using the same $16^3$ blocks as C1 but ranking by a ground-truth-free
signal~\cite{geifman2017selective}, we compare the net benefit recovered against the
oracle and a matched-random selector under a paired subject bootstrap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}).  Because the net benefit is $\approx\!0$ and its direction
barely predictable, recovery is weak: at the predeclared $20\%$ block budget the
entropy$-$random gap is $+0.23$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.06,+0.45]$) on 3D UX-Net and $-0.06$
($[-0.37,+0.22]$) on Swin UNETR---both CIs \emph{include zero}, so no deployable signal
beats random, whereas the oracle recovers $0.94$--$1.01$.  We therefore report a
\emph{negative} result at the matched configuration: entropy localizes where the scarce
flips occur, but there is no reliable net benefit to selectively recover.  Entropy,
confidence, and the boundary proxy are interchangeable here (their gaps coincide to
within $10^{-3}$), so we do not claim entropy is uniquely optimal.

\subsection{Mechanism: Which Factor Is Removable}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The comparisons above use end-to-end models, which share the encoder architecture but
not its weights. As a further, causally cleaner check, we \emph{freeze} one trained
3D UX-Net encoder and train a $2\times2$ decoder factorial on identical features,
varying only the two knobs \method{} changes at once---channel width ($C{=}384$
vs.\ $48$) and the high-resolution stage (resolution factor $r_f{=}1$ vs.\ $2$)---so any
difference is attributable to the decoder alone (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}).

Three readings follow. \textbf{(i)~The frozen-encoder attribution is valid:} the
full-decoder corner on the frozen encoder reaches $0.804$ Dice, matching (slightly
exceeding) the end-to-end full-UX model ($0.792$), so the frozen features do not
bottleneck the decoder. \textbf{(ii)~Channel width is the more removable factor:}
cutting $384\!\rightarrow\!48$ channels costs only $0.6$ Dice while removing
$95\%$ of decoder parameters ($46.7\!\rightarrow\!2.2$\,M). \textbf{(iii)~The
high-resolution stage carries the compute and most of the (small) accuracy cost:}
dropping it costs $2.5$ Dice and is where the MAC savings live
($657.8\!\rightarrow\!319.1$\,G at full width; $388.2\!\rightarrow\!49.5$\,G combined),
while leaving parameters almost unchanged---a near-pure compute/accuracy knob. The two
factors are largely independent: the combined \method{} decoder ($0.784$) tracks
resolution-only, with the channel cut nearly free on top. Because the frozen encoder was
optimized for the full decoder, the efficient decoder's near-match on those features is a
\emph{conservative} statement of redundancy. The corresponding per-factor \emph{flip}
localization---testing whether dropping the high-resolution stage concentrates flips
nearer the boundary than channel reduction does---is \todo{report from the pairwise
frozen-encoder flip analysis re-run}. We present this regime as validation of the decoder
attribution rather than as the primary result.

\section{Discussion: Implications for Efficient Decoder Design}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our characterization has three implications for how efficient 3D decoders are built and
evaluated. \textbf{First, aggregate Dice and FLOPs are insufficient summaries.} They
hide that the full decoder's advantage over a parameter-matched efficient decoder is, at
the voxel level, a near-perfect cancellation of improvements and regressions
($R_{\mathrm{net}}\!\approx\!0$): a decoder can differ by $\sim\!1$--$2$ Dice points while
providing no net voxel-level benefit. Reporting positive, negative, and net flips exposes
this and should accompany Dice/FLOPs whenever a decoder is simplified.
\textbf{Second, the redundancy is spatially structured, not diffuse.} The flips that
remain concentrate within one to two voxels of organ boundaries (their \emph{location} is
predictable from the efficient model's own entropy, AUROC $\approx\!0.9$), even though
their per-organ incidence is heterogeneous and their \emph{direction} is only weakly
predictable; efficient decoders can thus be designed and audited around a small,
identifiable boundary band rather than the whole volume. \textbf{Third, the redundancy is a property of the decoder, not a
single backbone.} Across 3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, and MedNeXt the same pattern holds---small
Dice cost, boundary-localized predictable flips, and a voxel-level net benefit
statistically indistinguishable from zero---indicating that the decoder stages removed by
\method{} contribute almost nothing to \emph{net voxel correctness}, even though, via that
same boundary band, they still account for a $1$--$2$ point macro-Dice gap. \method{}'s
aggressive compression is thus empirically well justified, and the removed capacity is
better described as net-neutral than as strictly unused. Future efficient decoders should
explicitly measure \emph{where} computation changes predictions rather than assume a
uniform accuracy cost.
