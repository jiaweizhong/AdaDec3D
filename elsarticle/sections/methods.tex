\section{Methods: A Characterization Framework}
\label{sec:methods}

To understand the spatial and functional contribution of decoder computation, we establish a controlled observation framework (Figure \ref{fig:framework}). This section outlines the controlled decoder interventions, voxel-level correctness framework, rate definitions, spatial block partitioning, and characterization metrics.

\subsection{Controlled Decoder Interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

We analyze decoder capacity by isolating two generic architectural factors that govern volumetric decoding computation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Channel Capacity ($C_{\text{dec}}$)}: The feature channel dimension across upsampling layers, controlling feature representation width ($C_{\text{full}} \rightarrow C_{\text{reduced}}$).
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution Decoder Stages ($RF$)}: The resolution scaling factor, controlling whether high-resolution upsampling stages (e.g., full resolution $1\times$) are retained ($RF_1$) or omitted ($RF_2$).
\end{enumerate}

Crossing these two experimental factors forms a $2\times2$ factorial intervention design (Full $E_0$, Channel-reduced, Stage-reduced, and Combined-efficient $E_1$). The specific channel-reduction ratios and stage-removal configurations follow the architecture-preserving decoder simplification strategy introduced by EffiDec3D \cite{effidec3d}. Interventions occur strictly within the decoder pathway (upsampling, skip fusion, and refinement) while keeping the feature encoder architecture untouched.

\textbf{Choice of Intervention Template}: We adopt the reduction principles of EffiDec3D as a controlled intervention template rather than treating EffiDec3D itself as the primary object of evaluation. Its decoder-localized and architecture-preserving modifications provide matched configurations in which channel capacity and high-resolution stage retention can be independently controlled. This enables spatial characterization of decoder contribution while minimizing confounding changes to the encoder family, prediction task, or training objective.

\subsection{Voxel-Level Correctness Activity Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

Let $v$ denote a voxel within a 3D medical image volume $V$, with ground truth label $y(v)$, full decoder prediction $\hat{y}_f(v)$, and efficient decoder prediction $\hat{y}_e(v)$. Comparing predictions between matched $E_0$ and $E_1$ decoders yields two correctness-changing outcomes per voxel (Figure \ref{fig:motivation}; see Supplementary Figures S1 and S2 for 3D UX-Net and MedNeXt-M-K3 visualizations):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive Correction ($P(v)$)}: $P(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) \neq y(v) \wedge \hat{y}_f(v) = y(v)]$, where the full decoder corrects an efficient baseline error.
    \item \textbf{Negative Degradation ($N(v)$)}: $N(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) = y(v) \wedge \hat{y}_f(v) \neq y(v)]$, where the full decoder alters a previously correct efficient prediction.
\end{itemize}

Defining voxel correctness indicators $C_f(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_f(v) = y(v)]$ and $C_e(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) = y(v)]$, we formalize two fundamental voxel indicators:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoder Net Gain ($G(v) = C_f(v) - C_e(v) = P(v) - N(v)$)}: Measures the net directional balance ($G(v) = +1$ for correction, $-1$ for degradation, $0$ for unchanged).
    \item \textbf{Correctness Activity ($A_{\text{corr}}(v) = P(v) + N(v) = |G(v)|$)}: Measures where additional decoder capacity alters prediction correctness (gross activity).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Global Rates and Evaluation Domains}
\label{sec:rates}

To aggregate voxel-level indicators into volume-level rates for subject $i$, we define the positive correction rate $R_{+}^{(i)}$, negative degradation rate $R_{-}^{(i)}$, gross activity rate $R_{\text{act}}^{(i)}$, and net gain rate $R_{\text{net}}^{(i)}$ over an evaluation domain $\Omega_i$:
\begin{equation}
R_{+}^{(i)} = \frac{1}{|\Omega_i|} \sum_{v \in \Omega_i} P_i(v), \quad R_{-}^{(i)} = \frac{1}{|\Omega_i|} \sum_{v \in \Omega_i} N_i(v)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{act}}^{(i)} = R_{+}^{(i)} + R_{-}^{(i)}, \quad R_{\text{net}}^{(i)} = R_{+}^{(i)} - R_{-}^{(i)}
\end{equation}

We evaluate these rates across two complementary spatial domains $\Omega_i$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{All-Volume Domain ($\Omega_{\text{all}}$)}: Encompasses all voxels within the 3D volume grid $V_i$, yielding $R_{\text{act}}^{\text{all}}$ and $R_{\text{net}}^{\text{all}}$. This domain measures aggregate computational efficiency over the complete volumetric field.
    \item \textbf{Foreground-Union Domain ($\Omega_{\text{fg}}$)}: Restricts evaluation to voxels belonging to ground-truth or predicted foreground structures ($\Omega_{\text{fg}} = \{v \in V_i \mid y_i(v) > 0 \lor \hat{y}_{e,i}(v) > 0 \lor \hat{y}_{f,i}(v) > 0\}$), yielding $R_{\text{act}}^{\text{fg}}$ and $R_{\text{net}}^{\text{fg}}$. By filtering out uninformative background voxels, this domain isolates localized prediction shifts directly on anatomical target regions.
\end{enumerate}
Global metrics are reported as population means with 95\% confidence intervals computed via 1000-resample paired bootstrapping across subjects, preserving subject-level correlation.

\subsection{Characterization Metrics and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:formulations}

We structure our characterization metrics along three primary evaluation axes: spatial anatomical heterogeneity, positive-gain concentration, and inference-time predictability (Table~\ref{tab:framework_summary}).

\subsubsection{Anatomical Boundary Stratification}
\label{sec:boundary_dist}
To analyze how correctness changes distribute relative to anatomical structures, we measure distance strictly to the \textbf{ground-truth anatomical boundary} (avoiding circularity from model-predicted boundaries) in physical millimeters:
\begin{equation}
d_{GT}(v) = \min_{u \in \partial S_{GT}} \| \mathbf{D} (x_v - x_u) \|_2 ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{D} = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ incorporates dataset physical voxel spacing. We stratify correctness activity $A_{\text{corr}}(v)$ and net gain $G(v)$ across physical distance bands ($0\text{--}2$, $2\text{--}4$, $4\text{--}8$, and $>8\text{~mm}$) to isolate partial-volume boundary interfaces from homogeneous organ interiors.

\subsubsection{Spatial Block Partitioning}
\label{sec:blocks}
To handle spatial sparsity and noise, voxels are aggregated into contiguous cubic spatial blocks covering approximately $24 \times 24 \times 24\text{~mm}^3$ (BTCV: $16 \times 16 \times 12$; MSD01: $24 \times 24 \times 24$; MSD08: $30 \times 30 \times 16$ voxels). A block $b_r$ is active if $A_{\text{corr}}(r) = \mathbf{1}[\sum_{v \in b_r} A_{\text{corr}}(v) > 0]$, and its directional gain is $G(r) = \sum_{v \in b_r} G(v)$.

\subsubsection{Gain Concentration ($K_{80}$)}
\label{sec:k80}
Sorting spatial blocks by descending net gain $G(r)$, the oracle coverage curve accumulated over positive net gain is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Coverage}(k) = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor (k/100) \cdot |B| \rfloor} \max(G(r), 0)}{\sum_{r=1}^{|B|} \max(G(r), 0)} .
\end{equation}
We report $K_{80}$, defined as the minimum volume fraction of spatial blocks an oracle selector must retain to capture $80\%$ of all positive net gain \cite{geifman2017selective}. Lower $K_{80}$ values denote higher spatial concentration of useful decoding work.

\subsubsection{Predictability Scores}
\label{sec:predictability}
To test whether cheap inference-time signals anticipate decoder utility, we evaluate single-pass Shannon entropy $H(v) = -\sum_{c} p_c(v) \log p_c(v)$ \cite{shannon1948mathematical,gal2016dropout,guo2017calibration} and $8$-view Test-Time Augmentation Mutual Information (TTA-MI) $I(v)$. We define two distinct evaluation tasks: \textbf{Location Predictability} (predicting active blocks $A_{\text{corr}}(r) > 0$) and \textbf{Direction Predictability} (distinguishing beneficial $G(r)>0$ from harmful $G(r)<0$ blocks) \cite{cmig2025quality}.

\subsubsection{Retrospective Dice Recovery ($R_{\text{rec}}$)}
\label{sec:recovery}
Given a selected block set $S_k$, we form a retrospective hybrid prediction $\hat{y}_k(v)$ that substitutes full decoder predictions inside $S_k$ while keeping efficient baseline predictions elsewhere. The fraction of recovered macro-Dice gap is:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{rec}}(k) = \frac{\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_k) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)}{\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_f) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)} .
\end{equation}
Evaluating $R_{\text{rec}}(k)$ across varying spatial budgets $k$ establishes an empirical upper bound on recoverable segmentation quality under spatial compute constraints.

\subsubsection{Factorial Factor Attribution}
\label{sec:factorial}
Under a frozen shared encoder, we evaluate a $2\times2$ factorial grid across channel width $C \in \{C_{384}, C_{48}\}$ and resolution scaling $RF \in \{RF_1, RF_2\}$. We compute channel main effect $\Delta_C$, resolution main effect $\Delta_{RF}$, and interaction $\Delta_{C \times RF}$ to isolate the architectural drivers of decoder performance.
