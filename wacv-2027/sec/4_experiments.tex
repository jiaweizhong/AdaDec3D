\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Experiment plan.}
Our study reproduces \method{}~\cite{rahman2025effidec3d} and evaluates it on a full
backbone $\times$ dataset grid (Table~\ref{tab:plan}).  For each backbone that
\method{} actually equips with an efficient decoder---3D
UX-Net~\cite{lee20233duxnet}, Swin UNETR~\cite{tang2022swinunetr}, and optionally
SwinUNETR-V2~\cite{he2023swinunetrv2} and MedNeXt~\cite{roy2023mednext}---we pair a
full and an \method{} decoder and run that matched pair on each of three to four
representative datasets from the \method{} evaluation: BTCV abdominal CT,
FeTA~\cite{payette2021feta} fetal-brain MRI, and predeclared MSD
tasks~\cite{antonelli2022msd} (Task01 BrainTumour, and Task06 Lung or Task08
HepaticVessel).  Every grid cell is therefore a matched full/efficient pair on which
the full metric suite is computed; cross-dataset consistency is read across each row
and cross-backbone consistency down each column.
UNETR~\cite{hatamizadeh2022unetr} and nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} are excluded
because \method{} builds no decoder pair for them, so a matched flip comparison is
undefined.

\paragraph{Dataset and split.}
The primary task is the 13-organ BTCV/Synapse abdominal CT benchmark with 18
training and 12 validation volumes: spleen, right and left kidney, gallbladder,
esophagus, liver, stomach, aorta, inferior vena cava, portal and splenic veins,
pancreas, and right and left adrenal glands.  Images are intensity-windowed and
resampled following the public \method{} pipeline; the same validation subjects are
paired across all model comparisons on a given dataset.

\paragraph{Two comparison regimes.}
Within each cell the full and \method{} models share the same encoder
\emph{architecture} and differ in the decoder (channels reduced to the minimum
encoder width, additive skip aggregation, resolution factor two).  We analyze them
under two regimes. (i)~\textbf{End-to-end (ecological).} Both models are trained
independently, so they share the encoder architecture but not its weights; their
flips reflect the deployed full-vs-efficient difference but conflate decoder capacity
with encoder-weight and optimization differences. (ii)~\textbf{Frozen shared encoder
(causal).} We take one trained encoder, freeze it, and train the decoder variants on
\emph{identical} features, so prediction differences are attributable to the decoder
alone. Both regimes hold the encoder architecture fixed, so the study characterizes
the decoder and draws no conclusions about the encoder itself.

\paragraph{Frozen-encoder factor decomposition.}
\method{} removes decoder computation in two ways at once---narrower channels and a
dropped high-resolution stage. To attribute effects, we decompose them with a $2\times2$
factorial on the frozen encoder, all within the \method{} decoder family so only the
two knobs vary: full (highest-resolution stage, wide channels), channel-only (wide
$\rightarrow$ 48 channels), resolution-only (drop the high-resolution stage), and the
combined \method{} decoder. Pairwise flips (Full$\rightarrow$channel-only,
Full$\rightarrow$resolution-only, Full$\rightarrow$combined) isolate which factor
changes which regions. The frozen encoder is optimized for the full decoder, so
``efficient still matches on frozen features'' is a \emph{conservative} redundancy
result.

\paragraph{Null-pair seed control.}
Because the net flip is tiny, we bound seed noise with a same-architecture null pair:
two Full models trained with different (predeclared) seeds. The claim is that
Full-vs-\method{} net flips and their concentration exceed this Full-vs-Full null.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
We reproduce the \method{} training protocol on a single NVIDIA RTX~5090 (32\,GB,
Blackwell) under PyTorch~2.6 / CUDA~12.8 with MONAI, using BF16 mixed precision
(no loss scaling).  Each model is trained for 45{,}000 optimizer steps
(validation every 250) with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$, a
Dice--cross-entropy loss, four $96^3$ random-crop samples per volume, and full
in-memory caching.  Sliding-window inference uses a $96^3$ window, batch size four,
overlap 0.7, and constant blending.  We evaluate one full run and two independently
initialized \method{} runs per cell to expose seed sensitivity.  Segmentation
quality is reported with Dice and 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), and
efficiency with parameters, multiply--accumulate operations (MACs), latency, and
peak memory.

\paragraph{Core quantities.}
Let $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)$, $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)$, and $y(v)$ be the
efficient prediction, full prediction, and ground-truth label at voxel $v$.  We
measure where the full decoder changes predictions with \emph{prediction flips},
the standard tool for comparing two classifiers on paired
data~\cite{yan2021pct,cormier2016churn}:
\begin{align}
P(v) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)\neq y(v)\ \wedge\ \hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)=y(v)\right], \label{eq:pflip}\\
N(v) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)=y(v)\ \wedge\ \hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)\neq y(v)\right], \label{eq:nflip}\\
U(v) &= P(v)-N(v), \label{eq:net}
\end{align}
a \emph{positive} flip (full corrects efficient), a \emph{negative} flip (full
degrades efficient), and the \emph{net} flip.  $P$ and $N$ are exactly the discordant
cells of the paired-agreement table behind McNemar's classifier-comparison
test~\cite{mcnemar1947,dietterich1998}; volume rates are
$R_{\mathrm{pos}}=\mathrm{mean}_v P(v)$, $R_{\mathrm{neg}}=\mathrm{mean}_v N(v)$, and
$R_{\mathrm{net}}=R_{\mathrm{pos}}-R_{\mathrm{neg}}$.  As test-time signals computable
from the efficient model alone we use predictive entropy
$H(v)=-\sum_{c} p_c(v)\log p_c(v)$, confidence $\max_c p_c(v)$, and Monte-Carlo
dropout variance~\cite{gal2016dropout}.

\paragraph{Characterization metrics.}
We group the analysis into three questions; each metric is computed per grid cell and
aggregated over subjects with a bootstrap, since the twelve validation volumes---not
the correlated voxels---are the independent units.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(1) Heterogeneity: is the benefit evenly distributed?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{O1, boundary error ratio.} Mean voxel error on the eroded boundary
  band divided by the eroded-interior error; ${>}1$ indicates boundary-concentrated
  error.
  \item \textbf{O4, per-organ difficulty.} Per-class Dice and mean entropy $H$,
  ranking the structures the decoder struggles on.
  \item \textbf{O10, size--difficulty relation.} Spearman $\rho$ between organ volume
  and difficulty, and the partial correlation of $H$ and error given $\log$-volume.
  \item \textbf{Boundary-resolved flips.} Positive/negative/net flip rate binned by
  Euclidean distance to the nearest GT boundary---testing whether the decoder
  \emph{benefit} (not just error) concentrates near boundaries. Complemented by
  Normalized Surface Dice (NSD) at 1--2 voxel tolerance for a surface-level view.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(2) Concentration: does a small subset carry most net benefit?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{O2, entropy sparsity.} $\Pr[H(v)>\tau]$, the fraction of high-entropy
  voxels.
  \item \textbf{O5, uncertainty--benefit correlation.} Subject-level Pearson $r(H,U)$
  between binned entropy and net flips.
  \item \textbf{O9$^{\text{orc}}$, voxel recovery.}
  $\sum_{\text{top-}k\%}P(v)\,/\,\sum P(v)$, the share of positive flips in the
  highest-entropy $k\%$ of voxels---a non-deployable upper bound.
  \item \textbf{O6, training persistence.} Mean entropy across checkpoints, testing
  whether concentration is a training transient.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(3) Predictability: can a test-time signal find the regions?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{O3, flip discrimination and calibration.} AUROC and AUPRC of entropy
  predicting flips~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}, and expected calibration error
  (ECE)~\cite{guo2017calibration}.
  \item \textbf{O9, opportunity curve.} A risk--coverage
  curve~\cite{geifman2017selective} over contiguous $16^3$ blocks (plus a $4$-voxel
  halo) ranked by entropy, reporting net-flip utility recovered versus a
  matched-random selector.
  \item \textbf{O11, routing-signal comparison.} Subject-level correlation of entropy,
  confidence, and MC-dropout with net flips.
\end{itemize}
