\begin{abstract}
The decoder is an essential module for fusing multi-scale features and restoring spatial
detail in U-Net architectures for 3D medical image segmentation. Recent designs, especially attention-based 
decoders, sharply increase its cost, and efficient methods such as EffiDec3D respond by shrinking high-resolution 
decoder stages---removing $>85\%$ of its parameters and
FLOPs across 3D UX-Net, MedNeXt-M-K3 and SwinUNETR with little loss in Dice.
This success, however, rests on the empirical assumption that decoder computation is
static and globally redundant; it does not explain \emph{why} the computation is
removable, \emph{where} the decoder still matters, or whether those regions can be
identified at test time. We present a systematic characterization of decoder
redundancy along three axes---\emph{heterogeneity}, \emph{concentration}, and
\emph{predictability}---by counting per-voxel prediction \emph{flips} between matched
full and efficient decoders, with a frozen-backbone control and a channel/resolution
factor decomposition that isolate the decoder path. We find the full decoder's net
benefit is small and \emph{bidirectional to the point of cancellation}: at the
parameter-matched configuration the net flip rate is statistically indistinguishable
from zero across matched backbones (e.g.\ $-0.001\%$ on SwinUNETR)---yet this voxel-level
neutrality coexists with a $1$--$2$ point Dice gap, because the balanced flips concentrate
at the Dice-weighted organ boundaries. Uncertainty reliably predicts \emph{where} the full
decoder changes a prediction (AUROC $\approx\!0.9$) but only weakly \emph{whether} the
change improves it (direction AUROC $0.56$--$0.59$): predicting instability is much
easier than predicting improvement. These trends are
consistent across architectures, indicating that the removed decoder capacity contributes
almost nothing to net voxel correctness while still influencing macro metrics through a
thin boundary band.
Our analysis provides an evaluation protocol and design guidance for simplifying and
auditing efficient 3D segmentation decoders.
\end{abstract}
