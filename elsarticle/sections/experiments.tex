\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section describes the target 3D backbones, clinical datasets, evaluation axes, and implementation setup.

\subsection{Target Architectures and Intervention Specifications}
\label{sec:backbones}

We evaluate matched decoder pairs across three representative 3D medical image segmentation backbones: a large-kernel CNN (3D UX-Net \cite{lee20233d}), a vision transformer (SwinUNETR \cite{hatamizadeh2022swin}), and a ConvNeXt-style network (MedNeXt-M-K3 \cite{roy2023mednext}). Interventions occur strictly within the decoder pathway (upsampling, skip fusion, and refinement) while keeping feature encoder architectures untouched. \subsection{Factorial Configurations}
\label{sec:factorial_configs}

To cleanly isolate the architectural mechanisms driving decoder activity, we perform a $2 \times 2$ factorial analysis on the 3D UX-Net backbone. By freezing the encoder and separating the decoding capacity along two structural axes (feature resolution and channel width), we evaluate whether performance and error variations originate from high-resolution spatial routing or abstract semantic capacity. Note that the factorial full configuration uses a separately instantiated decoder pathway to isolate these specific variables, and its FLOPs (657.76 GMac) are therefore not directly comparable with the tightly integrated end-to-end full configuration (578.74 GMac).

\subsection{Clinical Datasets and Target Spacings}
\label{sec:datasets}

Experiments are conducted across three diverse 3D volumetric medical imaging datasets covering different anatomical regions and imaging modalities. Volumes are resampled to target spacings to match physical block side lengths of approximately $24\text{ mm}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{BTCV Multi-Organ CT Dataset} \cite{landman2015btcv}: 30 abdominal CT scans (resampled to $1.5 \times 1.5 \times 2.0\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $16 \times 16 \times 12$ voxels) with manual annotations for 13 organ structures.
    \item \textbf{MSD01 BrainTumour MRI Dataset} \cite{bakas2018identifying,simpson2019large}: 484 multi-modal 3D MRI volumes (resampled to $1.0 \times 1.0 \times 1.0\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $24 \times 24 \times 24$ voxels) with three foreground tumor classes.
    \item \textbf{MSD08 HepaticVessel CT Dataset} \cite{simpson2019large}: 303 training volumes (resampled to $0.8 \times 0.8 \times 1.5\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $30 \times 30 \times 16$ voxels) focused on vascular and neoplastic structures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experimental Evaluation Axes}
\label{sec:protocols}

We evaluate decoder contribution along three complementary experimental axes rather than a full architecture-by-dataset Cartesian grid:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched full ($E_0$) vs. efficient ($E_1$) decoders across three backbones (3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, MedNeXt-M-K3) on the canonical BTCV multi-organ CT dataset to test architectural consistency.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Dataset Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched full ($E_0$) vs. efficient ($E_1$) decoders using a fixed canonical backbone (3D UX-Net) across three diverse datasets (BTCV CT, MSD01 BrainTumour MRI, and MSD08 HepaticVessel CT) to test modality and anatomical generalization.
    \item \textbf{Decoder-Isolated Factor Attribution Axis}: Evaluates a $2\times2$ factorial intervention design ($E_0$, Channel-only, Stage-only, $E_1$) using a frozen shared 3D UX-Net encoder on BTCV CT. Holding encoder feature representations strictly constant ensures that prediction shifts are primarily attributable to specific decoder interventions (channel width vs. resolution scaling).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training Protocols and Implementation Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All models were implemented in PyTorch using an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU with CUDA 12.8. Models were trained for 45,000 optimizer iterations using bfloat16 mixed precision and the AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate $1 \times 10^{-3}$ with cosine annealing, weight decay $1 \times 10^{-5}$) under a compound Dice-CrossEntropy loss function, evaluating every 250 iterations to match the EffiDec3D protocol. Sub-volume patches of $96 \times 96 \times 96$ voxels were extracted with an optimizer batch size of 1 volume per GPU, drawing 4 spatial crops per volume (yielding an effective batch size of 4 crops per optimization step). Standard 3D online data augmentations (random rotations, flips, intensity scaling) and dataset-specific intensity normalization were applied. Evaluation networks dynamically allocated a foreground sliding-window overlap of 0.7.

\subsection{Data Splits and Model Selection}
\label{sec:data_splits}

Every matched pair was trained and evaluated on identical subject-level training, validation, and test partitions, and partitioning was performed at the level of whole volumes so that no subject's sub-volumes leaked across splits. This shared-partition constraint is not a convenience but a requirement of the characterization: because our analysis is a \emph{paired} voxel-level comparison between the full and efficient decoders, any discrepancy in the underlying data split would confound decoder-attributed correctness changes with data-attributed variance. Holding the split fixed ensures that every flip we measure reflects a difference in decoder computation on the same input, not a difference in which subjects each model saw.

Model checkpoints were selected exclusively by validation macro-Dice rather than training loss, which avoids selecting an overfit iterate, and all subsequent characterization analyses were computed strictly on held-out test subjects that were never used for training or checkpoint selection. Confidence intervals were estimated by subject-level paired bootstrap resampling ($1000$ resamples) over the held-out test set: the same subjects are resampled jointly for both decoders, preserving the within-subject correlation that a paired comparison exploits, so the reported intervals reflect subject-level variability rather than the artificially narrow bounds that voxel-count pooling would produce.
