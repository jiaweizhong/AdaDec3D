\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section describes the target 3D backbones, clinical datasets, evaluation axes, and implementation setup. Our design triangulates decoder computation with three complementary probes on a shared 3D UX-Net / BTCV anchor (Figure~\ref{fig:design_arch} and Figure~\ref{fig:design_factorial}): an ecological \emph{generality} axis (matched full/efficient pairs across backbones and datasets), a \emph{causal} frozen shared-encoder $2\times2$ factorial, and a \emph{logic-robustness} control that replaces EffiDec3D's capacity removal with a capacity-preserving depthwise-separable decoder. The three probes differ in training regime (end-to-end vs.\ frozen encoder) and efficiency mechanism (capacity removal vs.\ preservation), so agreement among them is not built into any single intervention form.

\subsection{Target Architectures and Intervention Specifications}
\label{sec:backbones}

We evaluate matched decoder pairs across three representative 3D medical image segmentation backbones covering distinct architectural paradigms: a large-kernel CNN (3D UX-Net \cite{lee20233d}), a vision transformer (SwinUNETR \cite{hatamizadeh2022swin}), and a ConvNeXt-style network (MedNeXt-M-K3 \cite{roy2023mednext}). Interventions occur strictly within the decoder pathway (upsampling, skip fusion, and refinement) while preserving identical encoder weights and feature dimensions. For each backbone, the efficient decoder baseline ($E_1$) implements a standardized capacity reduction: an $87.5\%$ feature channel compression ($C_{\text{full}} \rightarrow C_{\text{reduced}}$) and the omission of the highest-resolution skip-refinement stage (Stage 1), following the architecture-preserving principles of EffiDec3D \cite{effidec3d}. All parameters, FLOPs (profiled on a standardized $1 \times C \times 96 \times 96 \times 96$ patch), and volumetric inference latencies are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}.

As a second, structurally different intervention template, we additionally train a \emph{depthwise-separable} decoder \cite{chollet2017xception} on the 3D UX-Net anchor. Unlike EffiDec3D, it removes no capacity: every channel width and high-resolution stage of the full decoder ($E_0$) is retained, and only each dense $3\times3\times3$ decoder convolution is factorized into a depthwise plus a pointwise convolution (reducing a dense $192\rightarrow192$ conv from $995$K to $42$K parameters, a $23.7\times$ per-convolution reduction). This lightens the decoder through an efficiency mechanism orthogonal to capacity pruning, and serves as the logic-robustness control of Section~\ref{sec:results_robustness}.

\subsection{Factorial Configurations}
\label{sec:factorial_configs}

To independently attribute decoder contributions to channel capacity versus spatial resolution scaling, we construct a $2 \times 2$ factorial grid on the 3D UX-Net backbone under a frozen, shared encoder. The grid crosses two channel capacity levels ($C_{384}$ vs. $C_{48}$) with two high-resolution stage retention levels ($RF_1$: retain full-resolution stage vs. $RF_2$: omit full-resolution stage). Note that while the end-to-end reference baseline (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}) incorporates auxiliary deep-supervision heads (578.74 GMac), the standalone factorial decoder isolates the decoding pathway with auxiliary heads detached (657.76 GMac under standalone patch profiling), enabling exact factor attribution without confounding loss terms.

\subsection{Clinical Datasets and Characteristics}
\label{sec:datasets}

To evaluate decoder computation across diverse anatomical targets, clinical tasks, and imaging modalities, we conduct experiments on three representative 3D volumetric benchmarks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{BTCV Abdominal Multi-Organ CT Benchmark} \cite{landman2015btcv}: Captures large-scale multi-class organ segmentation across 13 abdominal structures (30 CT volumes). It features diverse anatomical scales---ranging from large homogeneous organs (liver, spleen) to small, highly variable structures (adrenal glands, gallbladder)---with well-defined organ boundaries and organ-to-background interfaces.
    \item \textbf{MSD01 Brain Tumour Multi-Modal MRI Benchmark} \cite{bakas2018identifying,simpson2019large}: Captures neuro-oncological tissue segmentation across 484 multi-sequence MRI volumes (FLAIR, T1, T1ce, T2). It encompasses three sub-regions (edema, non-enhancing core, enhancing tumor) characterized by diffuse, infiltrative margins and highly irregular spatial topologies.
    \item \textbf{MSD08 Hepatic Vessel CT Benchmark} \cite{simpson2019large}: Captures fine-grained abdominal vascular segmentation across 303 CT volumes. It focuses on thin, tubular vessel branches and liver tumors, presenting extreme foreground sparsity, complex branching geometry, and high surface-area-to-volume ratios.
\end{enumerate}

Target voxel spacings ($1.5 \times 1.5 \times 2.0\text{~mm}^3$ for BTCV, $1.0 \times 1.0 \times 1.0\text{~mm}^3$ for MSD01, and $0.8 \times 0.8 \times 1.5\text{~mm}^3$ for MSD08) and physical spatial block partitioning follow the standardized protocol established in Section~\ref{sec:blocks} to maintain an invariant physical block footprint of approximately $24 \times 24 \times 24\text{~mm}^3$ across all modalities.

\subsection{Evaluation Strategy and Experimental Axes}
\label{sec:protocols}

To systematically map decoder contributions without evaluating an unconstrained, dense Cartesian product across all models and datasets, we organize our evaluation along three targeted experimental axes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched full ($E_0$) vs. efficient ($E_1$) decoders across three distinct backbones (3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, MedNeXt-M-K3) on the canonical BTCV CT dataset to establish architectural consistency across model families.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Dataset Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched decoder pairs using a fixed reference backbone (3D UX-Net) across three clinical benchmarks (BTCV CT, MSD01 Brain MRI, MSD08 Hepatic CT) to test generalization across imaging modalities and anatomical topologies.
    \item \textbf{Decoder-Isolated Factor Attribution Axis}: Deconstructs decoder capacity into a $2\times2$ factorial grid ($C_{384}$ vs. $C_{48}$, $RF_1$ vs. $RF_2$) under a frozen shared 3D UX-Net encoder on BTCV CT, strictly isolating channel capacity from resolution scaling without confounding encoder weight shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation and Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

All models were implemented in PyTorch 2.6 with CUDA 12.8 using an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. Training was conducted for 45,000 optimizer iterations using bfloat16 mixed precision, the AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate $1 \times 10^{-3}$ with cosine annealing, weight decay $1 \times 10^{-5}$), and a compound Dice-CrossEntropy loss function on $96 \times 96 \times 96$ voxel crops under standard 3D spatial augmentations. To guarantee exact paired comparisons, matched full and efficient decoders were trained and evaluated on identical subject-level volume splits. Model checkpoints were selected strictly by validation macro-Dice, and characterization metrics were evaluated on held-out test subjects, reporting population means with subject-level paired bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals ($1000$ resamples).
