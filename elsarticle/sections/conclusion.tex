\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a systematic characterization study of decoder computation in 3D medical image segmentation. Evaluating matched full and efficient decoder pairs across representative 3D backbones (3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, MedNeXt-M-K3) and clinical datasets (BTCV, MSD01, MSD08), we established that additional decoder capacity produces substantial gross correctness activity that is largely offset by bidirectional cancellation, yielding small net voxel gain. Spatially, positive gain is concentrated near ground-truth anatomical boundaries ($K_{80} \le 5\%$). At inference time, predictive entropy reliably locates active regions ($>0.90$ Location AUROC) but provides weak directional discrimination ($\approx 0.556$--$0.591$ Direction AUROC). Retrospective output-space hybridization recovered the full macro-Dice gap for 3D UX-Net and SwinUNETR on a 20\% block budget, while recovery remained partial for MedNeXt-M-K3. These insights establish essential characterization foundations, opportunity bounds, and design guidelines for future utility-aware adaptive decoders.
