\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results_discussion}

In this section, we present empirical characterization findings across target backbones, clinical datasets, and intervention designs, synthesizing numerical findings directly with their architectural, statistical, and clinical implications.

\subsection{Macro Trade-offs and Bidirectional Voxel Dynamics}
\label{sec:results_macro}

Evaluating aggregate segmentation accuracy alongside computational complexity reveals a well-known engineering phenomenon: decoder simplification yields massive computational savings at minimal cost to macro-level Dice scores. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, replacing the full decoder ($E_0$) with an efficient baseline ($E_1$) removes up to $91.4\%$ of FLOPs and reduces inference latency by up to $5\times$ (3D UX-Net: $40.6\text{ ms} \rightarrow 8.0\text{ ms}$; SwinUNETR: $37.0\text{ ms} \rightarrow 16.7\text{ ms}$; MedNeXt-M-K3: $27.8\text{ ms} \rightarrow 12.9\text{ ms}$). Across all three backbones, the corresponding Mean Dice penalty remains within $1.45$ percentage points ($79.18\% \rightarrow 77.73\%$ for 3D UX-Net, $80.48\% \rightarrow 79.49\%$ for SwinUNETR, and $82.61\% \rightarrow 81.35\%$ for MedNeXt-M-K3), while boundary error (HD95) even shows slight numerical improvements for two of the three backbones.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\columnwidth]{figures/h1_merged.png}
\caption{Global flip rates ($R_{+}$, $N$, $R_{\text{net}}$) across backbones under Full ($E_0$) vs. Efficient ($E_1$) decoders on BTCV CT.}
\label{fig:global_flips}
\end{figure}

Read at face value, these aggregate numbers appear to justify aggressive decoder pruning as a near-lossless optimization. However, a macro-averaged Dice score is a single scalar pooled over millions of voxels. Isolating voxel-level prediction shifts between matched decoder pairs exposes a fundamentally different underlying reality: macro-level metric stability masks extensive voxel-level prediction reorganization.

Evaluating voxel predictions demonstrates that decoder activity is governed by strong bidirectional cancellation ($P(v) \approx N(v)$), rendering aggregate net volume-wide gain statistically near-zero (Figure~\ref{fig:global_flips}). Positive voxel corrections $P(v)$ (where $E_0$ fixes an $E_1$ error) are substantially counterbalanced by negative degradations $N(v)$ (where $E_0$ degrades a correct $E_1$ prediction). Over the All-Volume domain ($\Omega_{\text{all}}$), subject-mean net gain rates $R_{\text{net}}^{\text{all}}$ remain minimal: $+0.046\%$ (95\% CI: $[-0.020\%, +0.110\%]$) for 3D UX-Net, $-0.001\%$ (95\% CI: $[-0.045\%, +0.040\%]$) for SwinUNETR, and $+0.052\%$ (95\% CI: $[-0.010\%, +0.108\%]$) for MedNeXt-M-K3. Even over the Foreground-Union domain ($\Omega_{\text{fg}}$), net gain rates stay small ($+0.381\%$ for 3D UX-Net, $-0.008\%$ for SwinUNETR, $+0.446\%$ for MedNeXt-M-K3), and all paired subject-bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals include zero. Rather than acting as a monotonic error corrector, high-capacity decoders perform spatial prediction reallocation whose net volume-wide effect is near-neutral.

\subsection{Spatial and Anatomical Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:results_heterogeneity}

The near-zero aggregate net gain is not the signature of an inert decoder, but of intense activity localized to specific spatial zones and anatomical structures. 

\begin{figure*}[tb]
\centering

\vspace{-2pt}{\small (b) SwinUNETR Organ-Level Flips} \\[3pt]

\label{fig:heterogeneity_anatomy}
\end{figure*}

Stratifying voxel correctness activity $A_{\text{corr}}(v)$ by physical Euclidean distance $d_{GT}(v)$ to the true ground-truth boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:boundary_flips}) shows a sharp peak within the innermost $0\text{--}2\text{ mm}$ boundary band, followed by rapid decay across $2\text{--}4\text{ mm}$ and $4\text{--}8\text{ mm}$, reaching virtual inactivity in homogeneous organ interiors ($d_{GT} > 8\text{ mm}$). Additional decoder computation is consumed almost exclusively in partial-volume transition zones where boundary ambiguity is highest. This spatial concentration confirms that feature representations within organ interiors are already well-resolved by the encoder, rendering high-resolution decoding pathways in interior regions computationally redundant.

This spatial pattern translates directly into an anatomical scale effect across organs (Figure~\ref{fig:heterogeneity_anatomy}). Small, morphologically complex structures with high surface-to-volume ratios exhibit the highest activity rates: left and right adrenal glands reach positive correction rates up to $16.9\%$ in 3D UX-Net ($14.8\%$ in SwinUNETR, $14.0\%$ in MedNeXt-M-K3), alongside high correction rates for the gallbladder and esophagus. Conversely, large compact organs such as the liver, spleen, and kidneys remain nearly inert (${<}3\%$ activity) because their volume is dominated by interior voxels rather than boundary margins. Thus, anatomical scale directly dictates decoder workload.

\paragraph{The Metric Weighting Paradox.} This anatomical separation resolves a key conceptual paradox: \emph{How can additional decoder computation improve macro-Dice while producing near-zero global voxel net gain?} Global voxel correctness is mathematically dominated by vast background regions and large organ interiors, where predictions are invariant to decoder capacity. In contrast, macro-averaged Dice weights all anatomical classes equally and is foreground-sensitive. Positive corrections concentrated on small, volatile structures (e.g., adrenal glands) exert a disproportionate impact on macro-Dice, even while global voxel shifts remain net-neutral across the full volumetric grid.

High-resolution stage retention ($RF_1 \rightarrow RF_2$) accounts for a major FLOP reduction ($657.8 \rightarrow 319.1\text{ GMac}$, $\Delta_{RF} = -338.66\text{ GMac}$) and controls spatial boundary refinement ($+0.033\%$ net gain in $0\text{--}2\text{ mm}$ band, decaying to $-0.011\%$ in interiors). Sorting spatial blocks by descending net gain (Figure~\ref{fig:concentration}) reveals that an oracle selector retaining just the top $5\%$ of spatial blocks captures at least $80\%$ of all positive net gain ($K_{80} \le 5\%$) across all three backbones, with the top $10\%$ capturing up to $99.98\%$. This confirms that the vast majority of decoder computation is spatially redundant, establishing the empirical precondition for budgeted, dynamic spatial decoding.

\label{sec:results_predictability}

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:predictability}, single-pass predictive Shannon entropy achieves high Location AUROC ($\ge 0.900\text{--}0.913$), reliably detecting active blocks. However, Direction AUROC drops to near-chance levels ($0.556\text{--}0.591$). Multi-sample epistemic disagreement via Test-Time Augmentation Mutual Information ($I(v)$) similarly yields modest direction AUROCs ($0.539\text{--}0.570$). Figure~\ref{fig:flip_density} exposes the physical cause: positive corrections $P(v)$ and negative degradations $N(v)$ share nearly identical conditional entropy distributions $p(H \mid P(v)=1) \approx p(H \mid N(v)=1)$, rendering them directionally indistinguishable from uncertainty alone. This exposes the fundamental \emph{Activity--Direction Predictability Gap}: predictive uncertainty identifies where computation alters predictions, but fails to predict whether those alterations will be beneficial or harmful.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{figures/recovery_merged.png}
\caption{Macro-Dice gap recovery trajectories under retrospective output hybridization across backbones.}
\label{fig:macro_recovery}
\end{figure}

Despite direction ambiguity, uncertainty locates activity well enough to guide spatial allocation. Under retrospective output-space hybridization (Figure~\ref{fig:macro_recovery}), substituting full decoder predictions in high-entropy blocks recovers $60\%$ of the full-volume Dice gap at a $5\%$ spatial budget, and achieves $\ge 100\%$ full gap recovery at a $20\%$ spatial budget across all backbones (versus only $17\text{--}20\%$ for random block selection). Crucially, because high-entropy spatial blocks concentrate the vast majority of gross correctness activity ($A_{\text{corr}}$), routing the full decoder to these candidate patches ensures that positive corrections $P(v)$ on volatile structures are captured within the retained $20\%$ spatial budget. This demonstrates that selectively routing computation to high-uncertainty regions can recover macroscopic performance while skipping heavy decoding across $80\%$ of the volumetric grid.

This critical finding proves that extra decoder computation is not monotonically beneficial, reinforcing the necessity of utility-gated dynamic computation.

\subsection{Clinical Stratification and Decoder Design Principles}
\label{sec:clinical_implications}

Translating these characterization findings into clinical practice yields a clear anatomical stratification for decoder deployment. Large, compact organs (e.g., liver, spleen, kidneys) are dominated by interior voxels and exhibit minimal activity (${<}3\%$), meaning efficient decoders can be safely deployed to maximize inference throughput in high-throughput screening or intraoperative guidance workflows. Conversely, small, morphologically complex structures (e.g., adrenal glands, gallbladder, vessels) exhibit high boundary activity (up to $16.9\%$), requiring high-resolution decoder capacity or targeted entropy-guided refinement for precise resection planning and vessel delineation. Finally, on thin vascular networks (e.g., MSD08), additional decoder capacity can overfit boundary noise, proving that computation should be gated based on expected gain rather than applied uniformly by default.

These empirical insights establish three practical principles for future dynamic 3D decoders:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Separate activity localization from utility estimation}: Predictive uncertainty locates candidate regions effectively but cannot predict gain direction; routing policies must pair location proposals with directional gain estimators to prevent harmful degradations.
    \item \textbf{Decouple globally shared decoding from local high-resolution refinement}: Channel capacity can be compressed globally with minimal spatial impact, whereas high-resolution stages should be selectively activated only on ambiguous boundary patches.
    \item \textbf{Treat additional computation as a task-dependent intervention}: Dynamic execution should default to a lightweight base decoder, allocating heavy high-resolution refinement only when the expected directional gain exceeds computational execution overhead.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While this study provides a comprehensive characterization of 3D decoder computation, three primary limitations should be noted:

First, our controlled interventions draw from architecture-preserving channel compression and stage omission templates (inspired by EffiDec3D and depthwise-separated decoder designs). Although validated across diverse CNN, ViT, and ConvNeXt backbones, future work may explore dynamic gating or non-uniform depth pruning to examine whether unconstrained architecture search yields different spatial redundancy patterns.

Second, selective spatial recovery is established via retrospective output-space hybridization. This defines an empirical opportunity bound for localized allocation rather than demonstrating an integrated, hardware-level dynamic execution engine with real-time runtime speedup and CUDA kernel acceleration.

Third, inference-time predictability was evaluated using single-pass predictive entropy and multi-view TTA mutual information. Exploring learned neural routing heads or feature-space gating mechanisms may further help close the direction predictability gap by learning to discriminate positive corrections from negative degradations directly from feature activations.
