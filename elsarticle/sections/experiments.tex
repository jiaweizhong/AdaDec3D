\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section describes the target 3D backbones, clinical datasets, evaluation axes, and implementation setup.

\subsection{Target Architectures and Intervention Specifications}
\label{sec:backbones}

We evaluate matched decoder pairs across three representative 3D medical image segmentation backbones: a large-kernel CNN (3D UX-Net \cite{lee20233d}), a vision transformer (SwinUNETR \cite{hatamizadeh2022swin}), and a ConvNeXt-style network (MedNeXt-M-K3 \cite{roy2023mednext}). Interventions occur strictly within the decoder pathway (upsampling, skip fusion, and refinement) while keeping feature encoder architectures untouched. Table \ref{tab:backbone_specs} details the intervention specifications and FLOP trade-offs across backbones.

\textit{Architectural \& FLOP Profiling Protocol}: All computational complexity metrics (FLOPs/MACs) were measured using MONAI's `fvcore` profiling utility on a single 3D patch input tensor of shape $1 \times C \times 96 \times 96 \times 96$. \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Reference Configuration} (Table \ref{tab:main_results}): Trained end-to-end with default MONAI training pipelines containing auxiliary deep-supervision heads (53.01M params / 578.74 GMac).

\subsection{Clinical Datasets and Target Spacings}
\label{sec:datasets}

Experiments are conducted across three diverse 3D volumetric medical imaging datasets covering different anatomical regions and imaging modalities. Volumes are resampled to target spacings to match physical block side lengths of approximately $24\text{ mm}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{BTCV Multi-Organ CT Dataset} \cite{landman2015btcv}: 30 abdominal CT scans (resampled to $1.5 \times 1.5 \times 2.0\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $16 \times 16 \times 12$ voxels) with manual annotations for 13 organ structures.
    \item \textbf{MSD01 BrainTumour MRI Dataset} \cite{bakas2018identifying,simpson2019large}: 484 multi-modal 3D MRI volumes (resampled to $1.0 \times 1.0 \times 1.0\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $24 \times 24 \times 24$ voxels) with 4 target classes.
    \item \textbf{MSD08 HepaticVessel CT Dataset} \cite{simpson2019large}: 443 abdominal CT volumes (resampled to $0.8 \times 0.8 \times 1.5\text{ mm}^3$; block dimensions $30 \times 30 \times 16$ voxels) focused on vascular and neoplastic structures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experimental Evaluation Axes}
\label{sec:protocols}

We evaluate decoder contribution along three complementary experimental axes rather than a full architecture-by-dataset Cartesian grid:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched full ($E_0$) vs. efficient ($E_1$) decoders across three backbones (3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, MedNeXt-M-K3) on the canonical BTCV multi-organ CT dataset to test architectural consistency.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Dataset Consistency Axis}: Evaluates matched full ($E_0$) vs. efficient ($E_1$) decoders using a fixed canonical backbone (3D UX-Net) across three diverse datasets (BTCV CT, MSD01 BrainTumour MRI, and MSD08 HepaticVessel CT) to test modality and anatomical generalization.
    \item \textbf{Decoder-Isolated Factor Attribution Axis}: Evaluates a $2\times2$ factorial intervention design ($E_0$, Channel-only, Stage-only, $E_1$) using a frozen shared 3D UX-Net encoder on BTCV CT. Holding encoder feature representations strictly constant ensures that prediction shifts are primarily attributable to specific decoder interventions (channel width vs. resolution scaling).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training Protocols and Implementation Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All models were implemented in PyTorch using an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU with CUDA 12.8. Models were trained for 45,000 optimizer iterations using bfloat16 mixed precision and the AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate $1 \times 10^{-3}$ with cosine annealing) under a compound Dice-CrossEntropy loss function, evaluating every 250 iterations to match the EffiDec3D protocol. Sub-volume patches of $96 \times 96 \times 96$ voxels were extracted with a batch size of 2 per GPU. Standard 3D online data augmentations (random rotations, flips, intensity scaling) and dataset-specific intensity normalization were applied. Evaluation was conducted using sliding-window inference with 50\% patch overlap.
