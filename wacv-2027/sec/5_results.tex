\emph{where} the full decoder's extra computation changes predictions---the question

\subsection{Heterogeneity Metrics}
\label{sec:heterogeneity}

With the parameter-matched \effimodel{}, the full decoder's benefit is small and
\emph{bidirectional to the point of cancellation}.  On 3D UX-Net, \fullmodel{} corrects
$0.278\%$ of \effimodel{}'s voxels ($R_{\mathrm{pos}}$) while degrading $0.232\%$
($R_{\mathrm{neg}}$), so the net flip rate $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is only $0.047\%$ and its
subject-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.026\%, +0.112\%]$ \emph{includes zero}
(Table~\ref{tab:flips}); on Swin UNETR the two rates are equal ($0.201\%$ each) and
$R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is $-0.001\%$.  Across both matched backbones the full decoder's net
benefit is thus \emph{statistically indistinguishable from zero at the voxel level}.
Crucially, this net neutrality coexists with the $1$--$2$ point Dice gap of
Table~\ref{tab:arch}: the flips, though nearly balanced in count, concentrate at organ
boundaries that Dice weights heavily, so the full decoder still moves macro metrics
through a thin boundary band even as its net contribution to voxel correctness vanishes.
Reporting positive flips alone would overstate a voxel-level benefit that, netted against
its own regressions, is essentially none.

We next ask whether that small net benefit is at least spatially concentrated (C1).
Ranking contiguous $16^3$ blocks by their true net benefit $B(r)=\sum_{v\in r}U(v)$,
the top $10\%$/$20\%$ of blocks recover \todo{$C_{10}$/$C_{20}$\%} of the volume's net
benefit, and $K_{80}=\,$\todo{}\% of blocks are needed to reach $80\%$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}, oracle curve).  The coverage curve is steep, so the net
benefit \emph{is} concentrated---but it is concentrated over a total that is itself
$\approx\!0$ (Table~\ref{tab:flips}), a small absolute opportunity rather than a large
one.  We report coverage and absolute net together precisely so the curve's shape is
not read as a large payoff.  For reference, a voxel-level oracle over entropy captures
$83.1\%$ of the \emph{positive} flips in its top $20\%$; but positive-flip coverage
ignores the near-equal negative flips that cancel them, which is why the net-benefit
oracle above is the honest concentration measure.

The scarce flips, though net-neutral, are not random---but only their \emph{location},
not their \emph{direction}, is predictable from the efficient model's own uncertainty
(P1, Table~\ref{tab:pred}).  Entropy separates any correctness-changing flip from stable
voxels (AUROC \todo{}) and predicts the \emph{positive}-flip voxel with per-subject AUROC
$0.903$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.876,0.926]$)~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}.  Restricted to the flip
voxels themselves, however, entropy cannot tell an improvement from a regression: the
positive-vs-negative (direction) AUROC is \todo{$\approx0.5$}, so knowing that a
prediction will change conveys almost nothing about whether it gets better.  Predicting
\emph{instability} is easy; predicting \emph{improvement} is not---the central obstacle
to turning this redundancy into a benefit-aware efficient decoder.  (Entropy is
moderately calibrated, ECE $0.088$, $[0.065,0.115]$~\cite{guo2017calibration}; the
pooled-bin entropy--error correlation $r=0.97$ is a descriptive diagnostic only.)

This unpredictable direction directly bounds deployable region selection (P2).  Using
the same $16^3$ blocks as C1 but ranking by a ground-truth-free
signal~\cite{geifman2017selective}, we compare the net benefit recovered against the
oracle and a matched-random selector under a paired subject bootstrap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}).  Because the net benefit is $\approx\!0$ and its
direction is unpredictable, recovery is weak: at the predeclared $20\%$ block budget the
entropy$-$random gap is \todo{$g\pm$CI} with a $95\%$ CI that includes zero
\todo{confirm from re-run}, and on Swin UNETR the paired lower bound is negative at every
budget.  We therefore report a \emph{negative} result at the matched configuration:
entropy localizes where the scarce flips occur, but there is no reliable net benefit to
selectively recover.  Entropy and confidence are interchangeable as signals (paired
difference $\leq0.02$), so we do not claim entropy is uniquely optimal.

\subsection{Mechanism: Which Factor Is Removable}
\label{sec:mechanism}

not its weights. As a further, causally cleaner check, we \emph{freeze} one trained
encoder and train the $2\times2$ decoder factorial on identical features, then compare
Full$\rightarrow$channel-only, Full$\rightarrow$resolution-only, and
Full$\rightarrow$combined \method{}

would explain why both are removable with little aggregate Dice change}.  Because the
frozen encoder was optimized for the full decoder, a near-match by the efficient
decoder on those features would be a \emph{conservative} statement of redundancy.  We
present this regime as validation of the decoder attribution rather than as the
primary result.

\section{Discussion: Implications for Efficient Decoder Design}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our characterization has three implications for how efficient 3D decoders are built and
evaluated. \textbf{First, aggregate Dice and FLOPs are insufficient summaries.} They
hide that the full decoder's advantage over a parameter-matched efficient decoder is, at
the voxel level, a near-perfect cancellation of improvements and regressions
($R_{\mathrm{net}}\!\approx\!0$): a decoder can differ by $\sim\!1$--$2$ Dice points while
providing no net voxel-level benefit. Reporting positive, negative, and net flips exposes
this and should accompany Dice/FLOPs whenever a decoder is simplified.
\textbf{Second, the redundancy is spatially structured, not diffuse.} The flips that
remain concentrate within one to two voxels of organ boundaries and on small structures,
and are predictable from the efficient model's own entropy (AUROC $\approx\!0.9$); efficient
decoders can thus be designed and audited around a small, identifiable boundary band rather
than the whole volume. \textbf{Third, the redundancy is a property of the decoder, not a
single backbone.} Across 3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, and MedNeXt the same pattern holds---small
Dice cost, boundary-localized predictable flips, and a voxel-level net benefit
statistically indistinguishable from zero---indicating that the decoder stages removed by
\method{} contribute almost nothing to \emph{net voxel correctness}, even though, via that
same boundary band, they still account for a $1$--$2$ point macro-Dice gap. \method{}'s
aggressive compression is thus empirically well justified, and the removed capacity is
better described as net-neutral than as strictly unused. Future efficient decoders should
explicitly measure \emph{where} computation changes predictions rather than assume a
uniform accuracy cost.
