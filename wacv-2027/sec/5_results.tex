We reproduce the matched \fullmodel{}/\effimodel{} pair and report its fidelity per
dataset (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}).  On BTCV our \effimodel{} reaches $0.777$ mean
Dice, $1.5$ points below the published $0.793$, while \fullmodel{} is within $0.6$

\effimodel{} matches its published $3.15$\,M parameter count, so the residual gap
reflects our reconstructed identity-affine data rather than the paper's processed set
(a FP32-versus-BF16 check ruled out a numerical cause).  Efficiency reproduces the
published reductions: \effimodel{} uses $11.7\times$ fewer MACs, $16.8\times$ fewer
parameters, $\sim\!5\times$ lower latency, and $\sim\!7.7\times$ lower peak memory.
The same pattern holds on the architecture axis (Table~\ref{tab:arch}): on BTCV,
Swin UNETR reproduces its published full model ($0.805$ vs.\ $0.8013$) and its
\method{} decoder cuts MACs $6.5\times$ at a $2.6$-point Dice cost---a smaller
reduction than 3D UX-Net's, matching \method{}'s own per-backbone figures.
MedNeXt-M-K3, the strongest backbone at $0.837$ full Dice, shows a $2.3$-point cost at
a $2.2\times$ MAC reduction; across the three backbones the Dice cost is small
($1.0$--$2.3\%$) even as the achievable MAC reduction ranges from $2.2\times$ to
$11.7\times$---consistent across architectures, not universal in magnitude.
Treating these matched models as fixed, the rest of the paper characterizes
\emph{where} the full decoder's extra computation changes predictions---the question
\method{}'s aggregate evaluation leaves open.

\subsection{Heterogeneity Metrics}
\label{sec:heterogeneity}

With the parameter-matched \effimodel{}, the full decoder's benefit is small and
\emph{bidirectional to the point of cancellation}.  On 3D UX-Net, \fullmodel{} corrects
$0.278\%$ of \effimodel{}'s voxels ($R_{\mathrm{pos}}$) while degrading $0.232\%$
($R_{\mathrm{neg}}$), so the net flip rate $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is only $0.047\%$ and its
subject-level $95\%$ CI $[-0.026\%, +0.112\%]$ \emph{includes zero}
(Table~\ref{tab:flips}); on Swin UNETR the two rates are equal ($0.201\%$ each) and
$R_{\mathrm{net}}$ is $-0.001\%$.  Across both matched backbones the full decoder's net
benefit is thus \emph{statistically indistinguishable from zero}---evidence that its
high-resolution, wide-channel computation is genuinely redundant, not merely reducible.
Reporting positive flips alone would overstate a benefit that, netted against its own
regressions, is essentially none.

This small net benefit is far from uniformly distributed.  As a first cut,
\effimodel{} error concentrates at structure boundaries ($4.39\times$ the
eroded-interior rate) and on small, hard organs---the adrenals, pancreas, and
veins---with net benefit rising as structure size falls (organ-size/net-flip
$\rho=-0.37$; Fig.~\ref{fig:organ}).  Resolving the \emph{net flip} rate by distance
to the GT boundary shows the decoder's benefit is sharply boundary-localized: it
peaks at the surface ($0.085$ at $0$--$1$ vox) and is near zero or slightly negative
beyond $1$ vox; surface Dice (NSD) is $0.741$/$0.845$ at $\tau{=}1$/$2$~mm.  This

A small, high-uncertainty subset carries most of the net benefit.  Predictive
entropy is spatially sparse: the median voxel entropy is near zero and only $1.26\%$
of voxels exceed $0.5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}a).  What net flips there are
concentrate in this tail---the subject-level Pearson correlation between binned
entropy and net flips is $r=0.639$ (95\% CI $[0.198, 0.987]$), bounded above zero
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration}b).  A voxel-level oracle makes the concentration
explicit: ranking foreground voxels by entropy, the top $20\%$ contain $83.1\%$ of
all positive flips.

\subsection{Predictability Metrics}
\label{sec:predictability}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{figures/O9_opportunity_corrected.png}
\caption{Region-level opportunity (3D UX-Net, concat). Entropy-ranked $16^3$ blocks
exceed a matched-random selector only in the mean and only at small budgets; under a
paired bootstrap the advantage is not significant at $\geq 10\%$ (and is negative for
Swin), because the net benefit itself is $\approx\!0$. Left panel is a non-deployable
voxel oracle.}
\label{fig:opportunity}
\end{figure}

The scarce flips that do occur, though net-neutral, are not random---they can be
located from the efficient model's own uncertainty.  We assess this as an
error-discrimination problem: predictive entropy should separate flip voxels from the
rest~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}.
Efficient-decoder entropy predicts the \emph{positive-flip} voxel with per-subject
AUROC $0.903$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.876,0.926]$) and AUPRC $0.299$ ($[0.259,0.342]$);
predicting the error voxel---a cheaper baseline---gives AUROC $0.856$
($[0.789,0.897]$) and AUPRC $0.530$ ($[0.499,0.559]$), and entropy is moderately
calibrated (ECE $0.088$, $[0.065,0.115]$)~\cite{guo2017calibration}.  The pooled-bin
entropy--error correlation $r=0.97$ is retained only as a descriptive diagnostic.

We then convert this into a deployable, selective-prediction analysis
\cite{geifman2017selective}.  Non-overlapping $16^3$ blocks are ranked by mean
entropy and expanded by a $4$-voxel halo; we measure net flip utility recovered
against a matched-random selector under a paired subject bootstrap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:opportunity}).  Because the net benefit is itself $\approx\!0$, this
recovery is correspondingly weak.  On 3D UX-Net, at the predeclared $20\%$ block budget
entropy-ranked regions attain a net utility of $0.161$ versus $0.035$ for random in the
mean, but the paired subject bootstrap is significant only at the smallest $5\%$ budget;
at every budget $\geq 10\%$ its CI includes zero.  On Swin UNETR the paired lower bound
is negative at \emph{every} budget.  We therefore report a \emph{negative} result at
the matched configuration: entropy localizes where the scarce flips occur, but there is
no reliable net benefit to selectively recover, because there is essentially no net
benefit at all.

Finally, entropy and confidence are interchangeable routing signals (subject
correlations $0.639$ vs.\ $0.648$, paired difference $\leq 0.02$); Monte-Carlo
dropout is inapplicable at this checkpoint's zero dropout, so we treat confidence as
an equally valid baseline and do not claim entropy is uniquely optimal.

\label{sec:mechanism}

not its weights. As a further, causally cleaner check, we \emph{freeze} one trained
encoder and train the $2\times2$ decoder factorial on identical features, then compare
Full$\rightarrow$channel-only, Full$\rightarrow$resolution-only, and
Full$\rightarrow$combined \method{}
