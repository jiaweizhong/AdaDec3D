\section{Methods: A Characterization Framework}
\label{sec:methods}

To understand the spatial and functional contribution of decoder computation, we establish a controlled observation framework (Figure \ref{fig:framework}). This section outlines the controlled decoder interventions, voxel-level correctness framework, rate definitions, spatial block partitioning, and characterization metrics.

\subsection{Controlled Decoder Interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

We analyze decoder capacity by isolating two generic architectural factors that govern volumetric decoding computation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Channel Capacity ($C_{\text{dec}}$)}: The feature channel dimension across upsampling layers, controlling feature representation width ($C_{\text{full}} \rightarrow C_{\text{reduced}}$).
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution Decoder Stages ($RF$)}: The resolution scaling factor, controlling whether high-resolution upsampling stages (e.g., full resolution $1\times$) are retained ($RF_1$) or omitted ($RF_2$).
\end{enumerate}

Crossing these two experimental factors forms a $2\times2$ factorial intervention design (Full $E_0$, Channel-reduced, Stage-reduced, and Combined-efficient $E_1$). The specific channel-reduction ratios and stage-removal configurations follow the architecture-preserving decoder simplification strategy introduced by EffiDec3D \cite{effidec3d}. Interventions occur strictly within the decoder pathway (upsampling, skip fusion, and refinement) while keeping the feature encoder architecture untouched.

\textbf{Choice of Intervention Template}: We adopt the reduction principles of EffiDec3D as a controlled intervention template rather than treating EffiDec3D itself as the primary object of evaluation. Its decoder-localized and architecture-preserving modifications provide matched configurations in which channel capacity and high-resolution stage retention can be independently controlled. This enables spatial characterization of decoder contribution while minimizing confounding changes to the encoder family, prediction task, or training objective.

\subsection{Voxel-Level Correctness Activity Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

Let $v$ denote a voxel within a 3D medical image volume $V$, with ground truth label $y(v)$, full decoder prediction $\hat{y}_f(v)$, and efficient decoder prediction $\hat{y}_e(v)$. Comparing predictions between matched $E_0$ and $E_1$ decoders yields two correctness-changing outcomes per voxel (Figure \ref{fig:motivation}; see Supplementary Figures S1 and S2 for 3D UX-Net and MedNeXt-M-K3 visualizations):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive Correction ($P(v)$)}: $P(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) \neq y(v) \wedge \hat{y}_f(v) = y(v)]$, where the full decoder corrects an efficient baseline error.
    \item \textbf{Negative Degradation ($N(v)$)}: $N(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) = y(v) \wedge \hat{y}_f(v) \neq y(v)]$, where the full decoder alters a previously correct efficient prediction.
\end{itemize}

Defining voxel correctness indicators $C_f(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_f(v) = y(v)]$ and $C_e(v) = \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e(v) = y(v)]$, we formalize two fundamental voxel indicators:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoder Net Gain ($G(v) = C_f(v) - C_e(v) = P(v) - N(v)$)}: Measures the net directional balance ($G(v) = +1$ for correction, $-1$ for degradation, $0$ for unchanged).
    \item \textbf{Correctness Activity ($A_{\text{corr}}(v) = P(v) + N(v) = |G(v)|$)}: Measures where additional decoder capacity alters prediction correctness (gross activity).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Subject-Level Rate Formulations and Evaluated Domains}
\label{sec:rates}

To aggregate voxel-level indicators into global rates for subject $i$, we define the subject-level positive correction rate $R_{\text{pos}}^{(i)}$, negative degradation rate $R_{\text{neg}}^{(i)}$, gross correctness activity rate $R_{\text{act}}^{(i)}$, and net gain rate $R_{\text{net}}^{(i)}$ over an evaluation domain $\Omega_i$:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{pos}}^{(i)} = \frac{1}{|\Omega_i|} \sum_{v \in \Omega_i} P_i(v), \quad R_{\text{neg}}^{(i)} = \frac{1}{|\Omega_i|} \sum_{v \in \Omega_i} N_i(v)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{act}}^{(i)} = R_{\text{pos}}^{(i)} + R_{\text{neg}}^{(i)}, \quad R_{\text{net}}^{(i)} = R_{\text{pos}}^{(i)} - R_{\text{neg}}^{(i)}
\end{equation}

We evaluate rates across two explicit spatial domains $\Omega_i$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{All-Volume Domain ($\Omega_{\text{all}}$)}: All voxels within the 3D bounding volume grid $V_i$, denoted by $R_{\text{act}}^{\text{all}}$ and $R_{\text{net}}^{\text{all}}$.
    \item \textbf{Foreground-Union Domain ($\Omega_{\text{fg}}$)}: The subset of voxels belonging to ground-truth or predicted foreground structures: $\Omega_{\text{fg}} = \{v \in V_i \mid y_i(v) > 0 \lor \hat{y}_{e,i}(v) > 0 \lor \hat{y}_{f,i}(v) > 0\}$, denoted by $R_{\text{act}}^{\text{fg}}$ and $R_{\text{net}}^{\text{fg}}$.
\end{enumerate}
Global metrics are reported as subject-level population means with 95\% confidence intervals computed via 1000-resample paired bootstrapping across subjects.

\subsection{Characterization Metrics and Spatial Partitioning}
\label{sec:formulations}

We evaluate decoder contribution across three core analytical axes (Table \ref{tab:framework_summary}):

\subsubsection{Physical Ground-Truth Anatomical Boundary Distance}
To prevent circularity from model prediction boundaries, spatial distance analysis is anchored strictly to the \textbf{Ground-Truth Anatomical Boundary} using physical Euclidean distances. Incorporating dataset voxel spacings $\mathbf{D} = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z)$, physical distance $d_{GT}(v)$ is defined by:
\begin{equation}
d_{GT}(v) = \min_{u \in \partial S_{GT}} \| \mathbf{D} (x_v - x_u) \|_2
\end{equation}
where $\partial S_{GT}$ represents the boundary voxel set of ground-truth annotations $\bigcup_{c} (y(v)=c)$. Correctness activity $A_{\text{corr}}(v)$ and net gain $G(v)$ are stratified across physical distance bands ($0\text{--}2\text{ mm}, 2\text{--}4\text{ mm}, 4\text{--}8\text{ mm}, >8\text{ mm}$).

\subsubsection{Spatial Block Construction and Physical Scale}
For block-level spatial analysis, volumes are resampled to dataset-specific target spacings (BTCV: $1.5 \times 1.5 \times 2.0\text{ mm}^3$; MSD01: $1.0 \times 1.0 \times 1.0\text{ mm}^3$; MSD08: $0.8 \times 0.8 \times 1.5\text{ mm}^3$). To ensure cross-dataset comparability, block dimensions are constructed to match physical dimensions of approximately $24 \times 24 \times 24\text{ mm}$ (BTCV: $16 \times 16 \times 12$ voxels; MSD01: $24 \times 24 \times 24$ voxels; MSD08: $30 \times 30 \times 16$ voxels). Block activity $A_{\text{corr}}(r) = \mathbf{1}[\sum_{v \in b_r} A_{\text{corr}}(v) > 0]$ detects active blocks. For directional utility classification, blocks with non-zero net gain $G(r) = \sum_{v \in b_r} G(v) \neq 0$ are evaluated for direction AUROC, excluding zero-gain blocks. Block-level metrics ($K_{80}$, Location AUROC, Direction AUROC) were computed within each subject and summarized across subjects, with confidence intervals estimated using clustered bootstrap resampling at the subject level.

\subsubsection{Positive Gain Concentration ($K_{80}$)}
Sorting blocks by $G(r)$, oracle coverage is defined by:
\begin{equation}
Coverage(k) = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor (k/100) \cdot |B| \rfloor} \max(G(r), 0)}{\sum_{r=1}^{|B|} \max(G(r), 0)}
\end{equation}
We record $K_{80}$, defined as the minimum percentage of spatial blocks required by an oracle selector to contain 80\% of the total positive decoder gain \cite{geifman2017selective}.

\subsubsection{Retrospective Output-Space Hybridization ($R_{\text{recovery}}$)}
To evaluate whether boundary-localized decoder improvements account for macro-metric gains, we evaluate a retrospective output-space hybrid prediction $\hat{y}_k(v)$, which substitutes full decoder predictions $\hat{y}_f(v)$ into selected blocks $S_k$ while retaining efficient baseline predictions $\hat{y}_e(v)$ elsewhere:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_k(v) = \begin{cases}
\hat{y}_f(v), & \text{if } v \in S_k \\
\hat{y}_e(v), & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
When $\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_f) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e) > 0$, we measure retrospective Dice gap recovery fraction $R_{\text{recovery}}(k)$:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{recovery}}(k) = \frac{\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_k) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)}{\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_f) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)}
\end{equation}
If the full decoder gap is non-positive ($\text{Dice}(\hat{y}_f) \le \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)$), absolute Dice difference $\Delta \text{Dice}(k) = \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_k) - \text{Dice}(\hat{y}_e)$ is reported instead. Values $R_{\text{recovery}}(k) > 1.0$ indicate over-recovery and are retained unclipped.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty Signals and Predictability Scores}
We compute predictive Shannon entropy $H(v) = -\sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c(v) \log p_c(v)$ from efficient baseline probabilities \cite{shannon1948mathematical,gal2016dropout,guo2017calibration}. Block-level predictor scores $H(r)$ and Test-Time Augmentation Mutual Information (TTA-MI) $I(r)$ are defined by block volume means:
\begin{equation}
H(r) = \frac{1}{|b_r|} \sum_{v \in b_r} H(v), \quad I(r) = \frac{1}{|b_r|} \sum_{v \in b_r} I(v)
\end{equation}
where $I(v) = H(\bar{p}(v)) - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K H(p_k(v))$ over $K=8$ spatial view transformations. All TTA probability maps were inverse-warped to the original image coordinate system before computing ensemble means and mutual information. Predictability is benchmarked across: (i) \textbf{Location AUROC/AUPRC} ($H(r)$ predicting $A_{\text{corr}}(r) > 0$), (ii) \textbf{Direction AUROC/AUPRC} ($H(r)$ predicting $G(r) > 0$ on non-zero gain blocks) \cite{cmig2025quality}, and (iii) \textbf{Conditional Density Overlap} $p(H \mid P(v)=1)$ vs. $p(H \mid N(v)=1)$.

\subsubsection{Factorial Main Effects and Interaction Analysis}
In our $2\times2$ decoder configuration grid, we quantify the channel main effect $\Delta_C$, stage main effect $\Delta_{RF}$, and channel-stage interaction $\Delta_{C \times RF}$ for any outcome metric $M$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta_C = \frac{1}{2} \big( &M(C_{48}, RF_1) + M(C_{48}, RF_2) \\
&- M(C_{384}, RF_1) - M(C_{384}, RF_2) \big)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta_{RF} = \frac{1}{2} \big( &M(C_{384}, RF_2) + M(C_{48}, RF_2) \\
&- M(C_{384}, RF_1) - M(C_{48}, RF_1) \big)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta_{C \times RF} = &M(C_{48}, RF_2) - M(C_{48}, RF_1) \\
&- M(C_{384}, RF_2) + M(C_{384}, RF_1)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
