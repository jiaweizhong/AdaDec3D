%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Modern 3D medical image segmentation architectures increasingly incorporate heavy multi-resolution decoders, implicitly assuming that dense decoding computation is universally necessary across the volumetric grid. Conversely, efficient alternatives simplify these pathways based solely on global accuracy metrics. However, where, how much, and under what spatial conditions additional decoder computation genuinely improves segmentation correctness remains uncharacterized. We present a systematic characterization framework for 3D decoder computation, evaluating matched full--efficient decoder pairs across diverse architectural backbones, multi-modality datasets, controlled $2\times2$ factorial factor attributions, and two distinct decoder-efficiency mechanisms (capacity removal and a capacity-preserving factorization). Our framework formalizes the separation between two quantities that aggregate metrics conflate: \textit{correctness activity} (where the decoder alters a voxel's correctness) and \textit{directional net gain} (the signed balance of corrections and degradations). We demonstrate that the two diverge sharply: across architectures and modalities, added decoder capacity exhibits high gross activity yet is nearly net-neutral, with corrections and degradations largely cancelling out over the volume. The residual gain is highly concentrated ($K_{80} \le 5\%$) and confined to a narrow $0\text{--}2\text{~mm}$ anatomical boundary margin. Crucially, while predictive uncertainty reliably locates \textit{where} correctness changes occur ($\text{Location AUROC} \ge 0.86$), it provides weak directional discrimination for \textit{whether} predictions improve ($\text{Direction AUROC} \approx 0.52\text{--}0.59$), establishing that prediction difficulty and computational utility are related but distinct. Clinically, these findings provide a stratified deployment paradigm: globally lightweight decoders can be safely trusted for real-time intraoperative guidance and bulk organ segmentation, whereas heavy decoding capacity can be sparsely allocated to critical anatomical margins to safeguard high-risk surgical and radiotherapeutic decisions.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Supplementary Material: Additional Implementation Details}

\subsection*{TTA-MI (Test-Time Augmentation Mutual Information) Setup}
For evaluating the predictive power of multi-view epistemic disagreement against spatial entropy, we utilized 8-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA). Each volume was forwarded through the network across all 8 spatial flip combinations ($x, y, z$ axes). The Mutual Information (TTA-MI) was computed as the difference between the entropy of the mean prediction and the mean of the individual view entropies. Despite this computationally expensive multi-pass estimation, TTA-MI provided only marginally better directional discrimination (Direction AUROC 0.570 for SwinUNETR, 0.539 for 3D UX-Net) compared to single-pass predictive entropy, further reinforcing our conclusion that uncertainty measures are powerful activity indicators but weak directional utility classifiers.
