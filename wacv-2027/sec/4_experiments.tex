\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Experiment plan.}
Our evaluation study reproduces \method{}~\cite{rahman2025effidec3d} and evaluates it with an
\emph{L-shaped} protocol (Table~\ref{tab:plan}) that separately tests cross-dataset
and cross-architecture consistency without an expensive full factorial.  A
\textbf{dataset axis} runs the matched 3D UX-Net~\cite{lee20233duxnet} pair on three
diverse tasks from the \method{} evaluation---BTCV abdominal CT,
FeTA~\cite{payette2021feta} fetal-brain MRI, and MSD Task08
HepaticVessel~\cite{antonelli2022msd} (CT, thin vessels/small lesions)---and an
\textbf{architecture axis} runs matched pairs on BTCV for three backbone families:
3D UX-Net (large-kernel CNN), Swin UNETR~\cite{tang2022swinunetr} (hierarchical
Transformer), and MedNeXt-M-K3~\cite{roy2023mednext} (ConvNeXt-style CNN).  The two
axes share the 3D UX-Net/BTCV anchor, which also carries the mechanism analysis
below.

\paragraph{Dataset and split.}
The primary task is the 13-organ BTCV/Synapse abdominal CT benchmark with 18
training and 12 validation volumes: spleen, right and left kidney, gallbladder,
esophagus, liver, stomach, aorta, inferior vena cava, portal and splenic veins,
pancreas, and right and left adrenal glands.  Images are intensity-windowed and
resampled following the public \method{} pipeline; the same validation subjects are
paired across all model comparisons on a given dataset.

\paragraph{Two comparison regimes.}
Within each cell the full and \method{} models share the same encoder
\emph{architecture} and differ in the decoder (channels reduced to the minimum
encoder width, additive skip aggregation, resolution factor two).  We analyze them
under two regimes. (i)~\textbf{End-to-end (ecological).} Both models are trained
independently, so they share the encoder architecture but not its weights; their
flips reflect the deployed full-vs-efficient difference but conflate decoder capacity
with encoder-weight and optimization differences. (ii)~\textbf{Frozen shared encoder
(causal).} We take one trained encoder, freeze it, and train the decoder variants on
\emph{identical} features, so prediction differences are attributable to the decoder
alone. Both regimes hold the encoder architecture fixed, so the study characterizes
the decoder and draws no conclusions about the encoder itself.

\paragraph{Frozen-encoder factor decomposition.}
\method{} removes decoder computation in two ways at once---narrower channels and a
dropped high-resolution stage. To attribute effects, we decompose them with a $2\times2$
factorial on the frozen encoder, all within the \method{} decoder family so only the
two knobs vary: full (highest-resolution stage, wide channels), channel-only (wide
$\rightarrow$ 48 channels), resolution-only (drop the high-resolution stage), and the
combined \method{} decoder. Pairwise flips (Full$\rightarrow$channel-only,
Full$\rightarrow$resolution-only, Full$\rightarrow$combined) isolate which factor
changes which regions. The frozen encoder is optimized for the full decoder, so
``efficient still matches on frozen features'' is a \emph{conservative} redundancy
result.

\paragraph{Null-pair seed control.}
Because the net flip is tiny, we bound seed noise with a same-architecture null pair:
two Full models trained with different (predeclared) seeds. The claim is that
Full-vs-\method{} net flips and their concentration exceed this Full-vs-Full null.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
We reproduce the \method{} training protocol on a single NVIDIA RTX~5090 (32\,GB,
Blackwell) under PyTorch~2.6 / CUDA~12.8 with MONAI, using BF16 mixed precision
(no loss scaling).  Each model is trained for 45{,}000 optimizer steps
(validation every 250) with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$, a
Dice--cross-entropy loss, four $96^3$ random-crop samples per volume, and full
in-memory caching.  Sliding-window inference uses a $96^3$ window, batch size four,
overlap 0.7, and constant blending.  We evaluate one full run and two independently
initialized \method{} runs per cell to expose seed sensitivity.  Segmentation
quality is reported with Dice and 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), and
efficiency with parameters, multiply--accumulate operations (MACs), latency, and
peak memory.

\paragraph{Core quantities.}
Let $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)$, $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)$, and $y(v)$ be the
efficient prediction, full prediction, and ground-truth label at voxel $v$.  We
measure where the full decoder changes predictions with \emph{prediction flips},
the standard tool for comparing two classifiers on paired
data~\cite{yan2021pct,cormier2016churn}:
\begin{align}
P(v) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)\neq y(v)\ \wedge\ \hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)=y(v)\right], \label{eq:pflip}\\
N(v) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat{y}_{\mathrm{E}}(v)=y(v)\ \wedge\ \hat{y}_{\mathrm{F}}(v)\neq y(v)\right], \label{eq:nflip}\\
U(v) &= P(v)-N(v), \label{eq:net}
\end{align}
a \emph{positive} flip (full corrects efficient), a \emph{negative} flip (full
degrades efficient), and the \emph{net} flip.  $P$ and $N$ are exactly the discordant
cells of the paired-agreement table behind McNemar's classifier-comparison
test~\cite{mcnemar1947,dietterich1998}; volume rates are
$R_{\mathrm{pos}}=\mathrm{mean}_v P(v)$, $R_{\mathrm{neg}}=\mathrm{mean}_v N(v)$, and
$R_{\mathrm{net}}=R_{\mathrm{pos}}-R_{\mathrm{neg}}$.  As test-time signals computable
from the efficient model alone we use predictive entropy
$H(v)=-\sum_{c} p_c(v)\log p_c(v)$ and confidence $\max_c p_c(v)$.

\paragraph{Characterization metrics.}
We organize the analysis as three questions and report exactly six metrics; each is
computed per grid cell and aggregated over subjects with a bootstrap, since the twelve
validation volumes---not the correlated voxels---are the independent units.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(1) Heterogeneity: is the decoder's effect uniform, or does
it depend on location and structure?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{H1, global flip rates.} The subject-mean $R_{\mathrm{pos}}$,
  $R_{\mathrm{neg}}$, and $R_{\mathrm{net}}=R_{\mathrm{pos}}-R_{\mathrm{neg}}$ of
  Eqs.~(\ref{eq:pflip})--(\ref{eq:net}), with a subject bootstrap CI on
  $R_{\mathrm{net}}$. \emph{Meaning:} whether the full decoder improves predictions
  uniformly or simultaneously corrects and degrades them---$R_{\mathrm{net}}\!\approx\!0$
  with $R_{\mathrm{pos}},R_{\mathrm{neg}}\!>\!0$ signals a bidirectional cancellation
  that a Dice gap alone cannot reveal.
  \item \textbf{H2, boundary- and anatomy-resolved net flips.} $R_{\mathrm{net}}(D_k)$
  over voxels binned by Euclidean distance $d(v)$ to the nearest GT boundary
  ($D_k\!\in\!\{0\text{--}1,1\text{--}2,2\text{--}4,{>}4\}$), and, on the
  $\mathrm{union}(\mathrm{GT},\mathrm{Full},\mathrm{Effi})$ foreground of each organ $c$
  (so false positives are not dropped), $R_{\mathrm{net},c}$ alongside the per-organ Dice
  change $\Delta\mathrm{Dice}_c=\mathrm{Dice}_{\mathrm{F},c}-\mathrm{Dice}_{\mathrm{E},c}$
  (NSD at 1--2 voxel tolerance as an optional boundary-quality column). \emph{Meaning:}
  \emph{where} the effect lives---whether the net benefit concentrates in a thin boundary
  band and in particular structures, explaining how a near-zero global net coexists with a
  macro Dice gap.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(2) Concentration: is the beneficial computation spatially
concentrated?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{C1, oracle net-benefit coverage.} Partition each volume into contiguous
  $16^3$ blocks, score block $r$ by its true net benefit $B(r)=\sum_{v\in r}U(v)$, rank
  by $B(r)$, and plot the cumulative recovered fraction
  $C_{\mathrm{oracle}}(k)=\sum_{r\in\text{top-}k}B(r)\,/\,\sum_r\max(B(r),0)$ against the
  block budget $k$ (normalizing by the positive net mass, not all positive flips).
  \emph{Meaning:} the ceiling---whether, \emph{with oracle knowledge}, a small region
  subset carries the net benefit. We report the absolute net $\sum_r B(r)$ beside the
  coverage so a steep curve over a near-zero total is not mistaken for a large opportunity.
  \item \textbf{C2, top-$k$ coverage.} The oracle coverage at 10\% and 20\% of blocks
  and the smallest budget $K_{80}$ reaching 80\% of the net benefit. \emph{Meaning:} a
  compact summary of \emph{how} concentrated the opportunity is.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(3) Predictability: can a test-time signal find the
beneficial regions without the ground truth?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{P1, flip-prediction AUROC.} Subject-level AUROC of the efficient model's
  entropy $H(v)$~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline} for three progressively harder targets:
  \emph{any} correctness-changing flip $P\!\vee\!N$, \emph{positive} flips $P$, and---
  restricted to flip voxels---the \emph{direction} $P$ vs.\ $N$. \emph{Meaning:} whether
  uncertainty predicts \emph{where} predictions change, \emph{where} they are corrected,
  and \emph{whether} a change is an improvement; a direction AUROC near $0.5$ means
  instability is predictable but improvement is not.
  \item \textbf{P2, deployable region selection.} Using the same blocks, rank by a
  ground-truth-free signal (entropy, confidence, a boundary proxy) and compare the
  recovered net benefit against the oracle and a matched-random
  selector~\cite{geifman2017selective}, reporting the paired (signal$-$random) gap with
  a subject bootstrap CI at the 20\% budget. \emph{Meaning:} whether the concentrated
  opportunity C1 identifies is actually \emph{recoverable} at inference---a signal helps
  only if its gap over random excludes zero.
\end{itemize}

consistency re-evaluate these six metrics across the rows and columns of
